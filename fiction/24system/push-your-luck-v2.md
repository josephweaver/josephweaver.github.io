# Push Your Luck v2

An experimental push rule for the [August playtest](2026-08-playtest.md). It uses the existing skill ladder and works with mixed-size skill dice as well; it does not require adopting build points or Background dice.

## What a Roll Represents

Your first roll represents your best reasonable effort: familiar methods, sound judgment, and the tricks you know, within the time and resources available. If it fails, simply repeating those methods does not earn another roll.

**Pushing means attempting a purely experimental technique beyond your reliable experience.** Describe what you change and what makes it dangerous. "I try harder" is not enough.

A novice still has many untried approaches that an expert would consider routine. The novice cannot reliably execute them, but those approaches may be comparatively safe. An expert's initial roll already accounts for those familiar options. After those fail, the expert must reach further into dangerous territory: bypassing a safeguard, forcing a delicate mechanism, or breaking a ritual boundary.

That is why smaller expert dice produce more complications when pushed. Expertise still makes the intended result more likely, but the remaining experimental methods carry greater danger. Natural 1s represent overreach, not a sudden loss of competence.

This interpretation does not grant unlimited time, equipment, or impossible capabilities. A changed situation can justify a new ordinary check; a multi-round task still grants its scheduled progress checks. Pushing is the optional dangerous extension of the failed check, not a requirement for every later attempt.

## Procedure

1. **Resolve the ordinary check**, including any Leverage the player chooses to spend. You may push only after failure, when a plausible experimental approach remains. The Guide may rule the situation settled.
2. **Describe the experimental approach.** Agree on an immediate minor cost and the consequences at risk. Costs must follow from the approach and the stakes.
3. **Push once:** Reroll both skill dice at the **same DC**, add the usual flat bonuses once, and replace the original result. The agreed cost applies regardless of success.
4. **Accept the pushed dice:** Leverage cannot be used on a push, even if you left rerolls unused on the ordinary check.
5. **Resolve the total and both dice** using the table below, including success benefits and complications together.

**No DC reductions:** Pushing and Double Down never lower the DC. Leverage changes die results, not the DC.

## Pushed Outcomes

Natural 1s are the faces showing on the dice, before flat bonuses. On a successful pushed roll, a maximum face still grants a **Partial Critical Success** benefit, and a total of **24** still grants a **Legendary Success** benefit. Resolve these benefits alongside the consequences below.

| Final dice | Total meets or beats DC | Total below DC |
|---|---|---|
| Neither die is 1 | Success; pay the agreed minor cost. | **Critical Failure:** fail and escalate the agreed cost to a Major Complication. |
| Exactly one die is 1 | **Partial Critical Failure:** succeed, pay the agreed cost, and suffer an additional minor complication. | **Critical Failure with an additional complication:** fail, escalate the agreed cost to major, and suffer an additional minor complication. |
| Both dice are 1 | **Legendary Failure**, regardless of total. | **Legendary Failure.** |

A **Legendary Failure** defeats the intended action and causes an exceptional disaster tied to the experimental approach, more severe than an ordinary Major Complication. It replaces the lesser failure entries rather than stacking every row. The agreed cost still applies, but do not charge the same loss twice. Legendary Failure does not automatically mean death; its consequences must fit the established stakes.

Success benefits do not cancel costs or natural-1 complications. A roll containing both a maximum and a 1 can succeed with a Partial Critical benefit and an additional complication. A maximum does not rescue a total below the DC; double 1s take precedence over the total.

## Leverage: Before the Push Only

**Leverage X** gives up to X optional single-die rerolls on the ordinary check. After seeing a result, choose either skill die, reroll it, and replace that die's previous face. Its size and flat bonus stay unchanged. You may choose the same die again if you have another reroll, or stop at any time. Always keep each die's latest result.

Only the **two final dice** determine ordinary-check critical triggers; a discarded maximum grants no benefit. A reroll can make the result worse. Finish all Leverage decisions before deciding whether to push; you need not spend every reroll, but unused rerolls cannot be used on the push.

**A push rerolls both dice once, with no Leverage rerolls afterward.** Neither spent nor unused Leverage is available, including on a Double Down push. A maximum on the ordinary check cannot be carried into the pushed result. An effect granting one advantage step supplies one Leverage reroll for an ordinary skill check in this prototype. Fear also grants Leverage equal to its current value on initiative rolls, using this same single-die reroll procedure; see [Fear and Initiative](fear-and-initiative.md).

On an ordinary check, Leverage can improve the total or seek a Partial Critical. Pushing commits you to the experimental attempt: accept its success benefits and consequences without further Leverage adjustments.

## Costs and Consequences

Choose costs from the fiction: **Position** (leave cover, lose balance), **Resource** (spend ammunition, damage equipment), **Exposure** (make noise, reveal yourself), **Strain** (lose stamina, gain Fear), or **Objective** (lose time, reduce quality).

- **Minor:** A setback preserving options, such as noise, a torn pocket, or losing 1 stamina.
- **Major:** An immediate serious problem or loss of control, such as being pinned, losing critical equipment, raising an alarm, or losing 4 stamina.
- **Legendary:** An exceptional escalation of the established danger, such as an experimental repair destroying the only working escape mechanism.

The player proposes the approach and cost; the Guide confirms or adjusts them before the roll. Additional complications should follow from the same experiment. Direct harm to another player character requires a risk made clear before the roll, a significant benefit, and table agreement.

## Examples

### The Expert's Dangerous Repair

An expert rolls `2d4 + 16` against DC 20 and fails. Familiar repairs have not worked in the available time. They push by bypassing a protective mechanism, accepting damage to their tools as the minor cost.

The pushed dice show `1, 4`: total **21**. The repair works, and the maximum grants a Partial Critical benefit: power returns faster than expected. The agreed tool-damage cost applies, and the natural 1 adds a complication: the repaired mechanism now makes a conspicuous grinding noise. The player must accept both dice; Leverage cannot remove the 1.

If the final dice were `1, 2`, the total would be **19**: Critical Failure plus an additional minor complication. If they were `1, 1`, it would be a Legendary Failure.

### Why the Novice Is Not Safer Overall

On a push at DC 20, `2d12` succeeds only **10.4%** of the time; `2d4 + 16` succeeds **81.3%** of the time. Every pushed miss is already a Critical Failure. Double 1s occur on **0.7%** of novice pushes and **6.3%** of expert pushes.

The novice usually fails the task and incurs a serious consequence. The expert usually accomplishes it, but their experimental overreach more often produces additional complications or an exceptional disaster.

## Double Down

**Once per scene**, after a pushed roll fails, Double Down permits one further push if a plausible approach remains. Resolve all consequences of the first push, including natural-1 complications or a Legendary Failure; the trait cannot undo them or override circumstances that make another attempt impossible.

Describe the further experiment, agree on a new minor cost, and reroll both dice at the same DC. Apply all v2 outcomes again, including success benefits. **No Leverage applies to this push either.**

## Playtest Notes

Record skill dice, DC, experimental approach, Leverage spent on the ordinary check, pushed total, natural 1s, maximum faces, success benefits, and consequences. Distinguish success with complications, ordinary pushed Critical Failure, and Legendary Failure. Track whether players understand why an expert's experimental options carry greater danger.
