## 1. Your Character


### Skills

Either select 1 rank 3 skill at d8d10+6,  select 2 rank 2 skill at 2d10+4, and 3 skills at rank 3 d10d12+2.  Or buy skills.

## Buy skills

Start with **12 build points (BP)** to spend across the eight skills below. Every skill starts at **2d12 for 0 BP**. Buy upgrades for either skill die independently; the dice do not need to match or follow the standard ladder.

| Individual skill die | Total BP cost (incremental upgrade) | Rank contribution |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------: | ----------------: |
| `d12`              |                                   0 |                 0 |
| `d10 + 2`          |                                   1 |                 1 |
| `d8 + 4`           |                               4(+3) |                 2 |
| `d6 + 6`           |                               9(+5) |                 3 |
| `d4 + 8`           |                              16(+7) |                 4 |

The first number is the total cost per die, equal to **that die's rank contribution squared**; parentheses show the additional BP needed to upgrade from the preceding die. Add both dice's costs for the skill's total cost and both flat bonuses for its roll. Upgrades require only the BP cost; there is no supporting-skill requirement.

**Skill rank:** Each single die-step upgrade increases the skill's rank by **1**. Add the rank contributions of both dice: `2d12` is rank 0, `d10 + d12 + 2` is rank 1, and both `2d10 + 4` and `d8 + d12 + 4` are rank 2. Rank counts upgrade steps, not BP spent.

**Costs:** `d12 + d10 + 2` costs **1 BP**; `2d10 + 4` costs **2 BP**; `d8 + d12 + 4` costs **4 BP**. `2d4 + 16` costs **32 BP**.

**Example starting allocation:** Melee rank 3 (`d8 + d10 + 6`, 5 BP), Athletics and Ranged each rank 2 (`2d10 + 4`, 2 BP each), and Lore rank 1 (`d10 + d12 + 2`, 3 BP) spend **12 BP**. All other skills remain rank 0.

Record each skill's rank, purchased dice, and combined bonus. Each skill describes a broad area of action:

| Skill     | Brief description                                                                |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Athletics | Strength, speed, endurance, balance, and movement through difficult terrain.     |
| Melee     | Close-quarters attacks, defense, positioning, and physical control.              |
| Ranged    | Attacks and tactical actions with bows, firearms, and other distant weapons.     |
| Thievery  | Stealth, infiltration, locks, traps, sleight of hand, and concealment.           |
| Lore      | History, languages, culture, religion, folklore, and general interpretation.     |
| Science   | Technical knowledge, investigation, medicine, engineering, and applied analysis. |
| Social    | Persuasion, deception, intimidation, rapport, and reading people.                |
| Magic     | Spells, rituals, supernatural effects, and the risks of wielding them.           |

**Choose Traits**

Traits define how your character approaches danger, survival, and problem-solving.

Choose **2 traits** from any categories below.

**Martial and Survivor Traits**

- **Sweep** - Once per round, as an `R(L)FH` action, attack 2 different melee targets at the regular DC instead of the Full Martial Attack DC.
- **Strong Back** - Carry 1 extra Large item without penalty.
- **Weapon Familiarity** - Choose one weapon type; gain one advantage step when using it.
- **Shield-Bearer** - You may shove or bash with a shield and still use `R` to Block once this round.
- **Tireless Runner** - Sprint and jog distance before stamina loss is doubled.
- **Elite Athlete** - Choose running, climbing, or swimming; increase distance by 50%.
- **Pack Fighter** - Gain one advantage step when adjacent to an ally attacking the same target.
- **Tough** - First time you are wounded in a combat, reduce that wound by 1.
- **Quick Draw** - Ready a weapon and attack in the same action.
- **Guarded Swing** - Once per round, if you used `L` for an attack on your turn, you may still Parry with `L`.
- **Acrobatic Finesse** - If you used `Sprint` or `Leap` this turn, you may still Dodge as a reaction..

**Social and Leadership Traits**

- **Commander** - Use your `H` action and make a Social check at DC `13 + the target's current Fear`. On a success, reduce the target character's Fear by 1 level; on a Partial Critical success, reduce it by 2 levels instead.
- **Psychologist** - Once per session, make a Science check at DC `18` to address one specific Bane affecting another character. Track successes across sessions for that character and Bane. After you accumulate 3 successes, that character can ignore that Bane at will.
- **Silver Tongue** - Once per scene, reroll a failed Social check.
- **Inspiring Presence** - Allies within earshot gain one advantage vs fear or fatigue checks.
- **Streetwise** - Gain minor hints or rumors when asking NPCs.
- **Menacing** - On a successful Intimidate, target hesitates (losses H action) for one round before acting aggressively.

**Survivalist and Explorer Traits**

- **Medic** - Use 1 medical supply to temporarily negate 1 wound penalty for a day. The character remains wounded but suffers no penalty for using that limb.
- **Keen Senses** - Guide always provides one extra environmental clue when you search.
- **Forager** - Roll a DC 16 lore check, if successful you negate 1 hunger and sleep penalty for the day.
- **Toughened** - Reduce stamina damage from environmental hazards by 1.
- **Pathfinder** - Wilderness travel time is reduced by one tier.
- **Trapwise** - You always notice at least one clue that a nearby trap exists.

**Mystic and Lore Traits**

- **Arcane Sensitivity** - You sense nearby magic, even faint traces.
- **Ritual Memory** - Perform one simple ritual per day without materials.
- **Scholar of the Old World** - Gain one advantage step on Lore checks about pre-disaster knowledge.
- **Dream-Touched** - Guide provides vague prophetic fragments during sleep.
- **Talisman-Bearer** - Once per session, negate a Minor Complication.

**Rogue and Trickster Traits**

- **Quick Fingers** - Automatic success on simple locks or simple traps under low pressure.
- **Shadowstep** - Gain one advantage step on Thievery in low light.
- **Evasive** - First hit per combat deals one fewer wound.
- **Deceptive Feint** - On a miss, target suffers `+2 DC` on its next attack against you this round.
- **Light Foot** - Ignore minor encumbrance penalties while sneaking or climbing.
- **Skulker** - Ignore low-light penalties and reduce no-light penalties by one step for Thievery actions.

**Dark and Gritty Survival Traits**

- **Foolhardy** - You rarely appreciate danger until it is already happening. Reduce incoming Fear by 1.
- **Cowardly** - Your imagination races ahead of you. Increase Fear gained by 1.
- **Brave** - Use your `H` action to treat your Fear as 2 levels lower for determining movement restrictions for the remainder of your turn.
- **Scarred Survivor** - After a Major Complication, gain one advantage on your next action.
- **Double Down** - Once per scene, after a pushed roll fails, resolve all its consequences, then push once more if a plausible experimental approach remains. Agree on a new minor cost and reroll both dice at the same DC using [Push Your Luck v2](push-your-luck-v2.md). No Leverage applies to either push; this cannot undo consequences or make an impossible attempt possible.
- **Desperate Strength** - When at negative stamina, your first successful attack in a scene gains +1 wound.
- **Grim Reputation** - Gain one advantage step on Intimidate; suffer `+2 DC` on Persuade against neutral or friendly targets.
- **Bloodied but Unbowed** - First wound in combat causes you to gain 1 stamina.
- **Diehard** - Roll `1d12` instead of `1d6` to determine your death threshold from bleeding after unconsciousness: **-5 - 1d12 stamina**.

### Gaining Traits

Each trait cost is based on $(\text{Current \# of Traits} + 1 ) \times 2$.  That is say you want to buy your third trait it costs $(2+1)\times 2=6$. To buy your 4th trait it would cost 8 and so forth.

## 2. Making a Check

### Core Resolution Ladder

The standard step-down progression from [core.md](core.md#skill-rank-dice-progression), extended through d4, alternates upgrades between the two dice. Other combinations are allowed at the same rank. Costs below are the combined squared costs of the two dice.

| Rank | Standard roll     | Average Score | Total BP cost |
| ---: | ----------------- | ------------: | ------------: |
|    0 | `2d12`          |            13 |             0 |
|    1 | `d10 + d12 + 2` |            14 |             1 |
|    2 | `2d10 + 4`      |            15 |             2 |
|    3 | `d8 + d10 + 6`  |            16 |             5 |
|    4 | `2d8 + 8`       |            17 |             8 |
|    5 | `d6 + d8 + 10`  |            18 |            13 |
|    6 | `2d6 + 12`      |            19 |            18 |
|    7 | `d4 + d6 + 14`  |            20 |            25 |
|    8 | `2d4 + 16`      |            21 |            32 |

Every combination at rank R has an average of **13 + R**. For example, rank 2 can be `2d10 + 4` (2 BP) or `d8 + d12 + 4` (4 BP); both average 15. Roll the dice actually purchased, not automatically the standard combination at that rank.

### Dice Resolution

1. Choose the skill and read its purchased dice and bonuses.
2. Roll those two dice and add their combined flat bonus once.
3. **Leverage X** grants up to X optional single-die rerolls on an ordinary check. After seeing the dice, choose either die to reroll and keep its new result, even if worse. You may reroll the same die again or stop. Add flat bonuses only once. Finish Leverage decisions before deciding to push; no Leverage applies to pushed rolls. One advantage step grants one such reroll on an ordinary skill check. Fear grants Leverage equal to its current value on initiative rolls, using the same reroll procedure.
4. Compare the result to the Difficulty (DC). Meeting or beating the DC succeeds.

On an ordinary check, if either final die shows its maximum value, the roll is a **Partial Critical** and gains a small additional benefit. A final result of `24` is a **Legendary Success** and gains a major additional benefit. Discarded results do not trigger critical effects. Pushed checks use the exceptions below.

If the result is below the DC, the result is considered no effect, no progress, or otherwise a failure.  The is no other consequence, unless...

### Push Your Luck

Your first roll represents your best reasonable effort, including familiar methods and the tricks you know within the available time and resources. After failure, **pushing means trying an experimental technique beyond your reliable experience**. Describe the dangerous change; repeating the same method or saying "I try harder" is not enough.

A novice has many untried approaches an expert would consider routine. An expert's first roll already accounts for those options; what remains reaches further into dangerous territory. Smaller expert dice make success more reliable but pushed complications more frequent: **overreach, not lost competence**. Scheduled multi-round progress checks remain ordinary checks; pushing is optional.

**Procedure:** After a failed check and all ordinary-check Leverage decisions, agree with the Guide on the experimental approach, an immediate minor cost, and the consequences at risk. **Push once:** reroll both skill dice at the **same DC**, keeping the new result and usual flat bonuses. **No Leverage rerolls apply**, even if some were unused; accept both pushed dice. This also applies to Double Down.

**No DC reductions:** Neither pushing nor Double Down lowers the DC. Leverage changes die results, not the DC.

| Final pushed result     | Outcome                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meets DC, no natural 1s | Success plus the agreed minor cost.                                                                                                                                                       |
| Below DC, no natural 1s | **Critical Failure:** fail and escalate the agreed cost to a Major Complication.                                                                                                    |
| Exactly one natural 1   | Add a minor complication to either result above; success becomes a**Partial Critical Failure** that still achieves the action.                                                      |
| Double 1s               | **Legendary Failure:** the action fails regardless of total, with an exceptional disaster tied to the approach. Replaces lesser failure effects; do not charge the same cost twice. |

**Success benefits remain:** On a successful push, a maximum face grants a **Partial Critical Success** benefit, and a total of 24 grants the **Legendary Success** benefit. Benefits and complications can occur together; neither cancels the other or the agreed cost. A maximum does not rescue a total below the DC, and double 1s override the total.

**Costs:** Position, Resource, Exposure, Strain, or Objective. Minor = a setback preserving options (noise, torn pocket, lose 1 stamina); major = a serious immediate problem (pinned, critical gear lost, alarm, lose 4 stamina). Legendary consequences must fit the established stakes, not automatically cause death. Direct harm to another PC requires a risk clear before rolling, significant benefit, and table agreement. **Double Down** permits one further push as described in its trait.

**Example:** An expert fails a DC 20 repair and pushes by bypassing a safeguard, risking their tools. Their `2d4 + 16` shows `1, 4`: total 21. The repair works; the maximum restores power faster than expected, while the 1 makes the mechanism grind noisily. The agreed tool-damage cost also applies. No Leverage can change the pushed dice. A `1, 2` instead fails with a Major Complication plus an additional minor complication; `1, 1` is Legendary Failure.

See [Push Your Luck v2](push-your-luck-v2.md) for the full rule and design explanation.

### Setting the DC

Use two different methods:

- **Contested roll:** The DC is the opposing character's average result for the relevant skill (**13 + skill rank**). The acting character must meet or beat that average.
- **Unopposed roll:** Use either a one-round action or a multi-round action, as described below.

#### One-Round Actions

Use one roll when the action resolves in a single round. Set the DC from the table below, then apply any Pressure or other situational modifiers.

| Difficulty  | DC | Example                                                       |
| ----------- | -: | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Easy        |  8 | A small pit or a straightforward action under pressure.       |
| Medium      | 10 | A routine challenge with meaningful uncertainty.              |
| Hard        | 12 | A medium pit or a demanding action.                           |
| Very Hard   | 14 | A severe challenge requiring strong skill or good conditions. |
| Confounding | 16 | An extreme action that a specialist may still fail.           |
| Scary       | 18 | A challenge with very little room for error.                  |
| Legendary   | 20 | An action that borders on impossible.                         |

#### Multi-Round Actions

Use a multi-round action when success requires time, concentration, or repeated progress.

- The **DC** measures how difficult it is to make progress in a single round.
- The **required successes** measure the total size and duration of the task.

Each round, the player makes one check against the progress DC. A success marks one progress success. An ordinary failure only consumes that round. The player may Push Their Luck as normal, accepting the usual complication risk.

#### Example: Picking a Lock

Exploration is moving in ten-minute moments. The Guide decides that picking this lock is a Short task requiring `2` successes. Making progress on the lock is DC `14`.

## 3. Taking Action

### The RHLF (“Ralph”) Action System

The 24-System uses a **body-anchored action economy** called the **RHLF Action System**, informally known as the **“Ralph” system**.
Rather than abstract action points, characters commit **parts of their body** to perform actions.

RHLF stands for:

- **L** – Primary Hand (the hand holding the weapon)
- **R** – Secondary Hand (optional or supporting)
- **H** – Head (awareness, targeting, balance, intent)
- **F** – Feet (stance, movement, positioning)

These anchors determine **what actions are possible**, **what bonuses apply**, and **what happens when a character is injured or exhausted**.

Action Commitment

Every action specifies which body anchors it requires.

Examples:

- `L` – One-handed action
- `LH` – One-handed action with focus or targeting
- `(R)LHF` – A fully committed martial action using:
  - Primary hand
  - Optional secondary hand (two-handed weapon)
  - Head (awareness, timing)
  - Feet (stance and balance)

**Parentheses indicate optional commitment**, usually for two-handed weapons or heavier techniques.

### Fear and Initiative

Abbreviated from [Fear and Initiative](fear-and-initiative.md). Track **Fear from 0 to 6**: it speeds reactions but restricts actions.

**Combat start:**

1. Every character and monster rolls **`2d12` with Leverage equal to their current Fear**. Finish the optional single-die rerolls, keeping each new result even if worse. Record the two final dice and their total, including flat initiative modifiers once.
2. Resolve **enemy initiative critical moves first**, recording the Fear they cause.
3. Resolve **player initiative critical moves next**, making any required Athletics checks and recording Fear reductions.
4. **Set new Fear levels** by applying the recorded gains and reductions, with traits and Banes. Keep tracked Fear at a minimum of 0 and resolve any mental break at Fear 6.
5. Use the **recorded initiative scores**, highest to lowest, to decide who goes first. **Players win ties against monsters**; tied players decide their order among themselves, and the Guide orders tied monsters.

**Initiative modifiers:** Surprised `-2`, Low Light `-1`, Darkness `-2`, Distracted `-1`, Severely Distracted `-2`. Applicable flat modifiers stack and apply once; Fear supplies Leverage.

**Initiative criticals:** A final d12 showing **12** grants a Partial Critical; a final initiative total of **24** grants a Legendary. Discarded dice do not count. Resolve these during combat setup, enemies before players. Use the Legendary outcome instead of the Partial Critical outcome.

| Combatant        | Partial Critical                                                                                          | Legendary                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monster          | Move to**Melee** with a chosen character, if possible; record **+2 Fear** for that character. | Move to**Grappling** with a chosen character, if possible; record **+4 Fear** for that character. |
| Player character | Move up to**MR**, making any required **Athletics checks**; record **-2 Fear**.         | Move up to**twice MR**, making any required **Athletics checks**; record **-4 Fear**.       |

This bonus movement does not spend the normal `F` action. [Normal movement rules](movement.md) for terrain, Guard, engagement, injury, and current Fear restrictions apply. A monster already at the indicated range or closer does not move away; record its Fear effect even if movement is blocked or unnecessary. After both groups finish moving, apply each character's net Fear change, applying Fear traits to incoming Fear and retaining applicable Bane modifiers.

Actions [readied before combat](#interrupts-and-follow-ups) can trigger during setup movement. Resolve them at their declared Interrupt or Follow-up timing, then continue setup.

Use Fear at the beginning of the roll to determine Leverage. Updated Fear affects subsequent actions; the recorded initiative scores stay the same. Once turns begin, follow the order in a cycle. Later Fear changes update restrictions while the current order continues.

**Act before being attacked:** At the start of your first turn in combat, reduce Fear by **1** if no attack has been attempted against you since combat began, even an attack that missed. This benefit applies once per combat.

**Fear restrictions:** Use the current row; Pressure values are totals, not cumulative. Concentration Pressure applies to Lore, Science, Social, and Magic; hostile-action Pressure applies to Melee and Ranged actions against sources of Fear.

| Fear | State        | Concentration Pressure | Hostile-action Pressure | Restriction                                                |
| ---: | ------------ | ---------------------: | ----------------------: | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
|    0 | Steady       |                      0 |                       0 | None.                                                      |
|    1 | Unease       |                      0 |                       0 | None.                                                      |
|    2 | Tension      |                      1 |                       0 | Half-speed movement toward the controlling source.         |
|    3 | Strain       |                      1 |                       1 | Cannot move toward the controlling source.                 |
|    4 | Panic        |                      2 |                       2 | Cannot approach; move at least one step away on your turn. |
|    5 | Overwhelm    |                      3 |                       3 | Cannot approach; use all available movement to move away.  |
|    6 | Mental Break |                     — |                      — | Immediately reset Fear to 0 and gain a permanent Bane.     |

**Controlling source:** Only the source with the highest Fear rating determines movement direction; ties use the most recently encountered source. A newer, weaker source adds Fear without changing that direction. Total current Fear determines restriction severity; hostile-action Pressure still applies against all sources of Fear.

**Recovery:** In the round immediately after gaining Fear, spend a full `RLHF` action to reduce Fear by **1**, at most **once per scene**. The opportunity expires after that round; gaining Fear again opens another opportunity but does not reset the once-per-scene limit. Ending a scene does not automatically remove Fear. A **full rest resets Fear to 0**. Recovery changes restrictions immediately but does not reroll initiative. Fear traits above apply normally.

Initiative critical benefits and acting before being attacked reduce Fear separately, without using the recovery action or its once-per-scene allowance.

**Madness and Banes:** At Fear 6, immediately reset Fear to 0 and gain a permanent Bane tied to the source. During combat setup, resolve the break when setting new Fear levels, before using the recorded scores to determine turn order. Later breaks resolve when the threshold is reached while the current order continues. **Madness and Banes are cumulative:** each break adds a Bane, including another instance of the same Bane, and all applicable effects stack. Each Fear-increasing Bane adds **+1 Fear** while its trigger is present. Two breaks tied to small dogs give **Fear of Small Dogs 2**, adding **+2 Fear** while small dogs are present. Different triggered Fear Banes also add together. Record triggers, effects, and counts. These modifiers affect initiative and restrictions without adding another point each round. Fear reductions and resets lower tracked Fear to a minimum of 0; Banes survive rest and recovery, and their triggered modifiers still apply after a reset.

**Delay:** On your turn, spend one available `RHLF` anchor to move to a later position in the circular order, then act there with your remaining anchors and current Fear restrictions. Resolve start-of-turn effects only at your original position.

### Defence

One defence per incoming attack, required limb must be uncommitted this round unless a trait/weapon rule overrides. Normal movement preserves Dodge; Sprint/Leap prevents it until your next turn unless a trait overrides. Attack must meet or beat defence DC; cover/concealment modifiers apply afterward.

**No Active Defence:** Attack DC `13`, normal damage on hit.

**Dodge (`F`):** Attack DC = average Athletics, avoids all damage on a miss, always costs stamina; on hit, stamina spent + normal damage.

**Parry (`L`):** Weapon required, attack DC = average Melee, no stamina cost. On hit, Guide reduces/redirects damage: -1 wound, weapon/armor integrity loss, or lost balance/position. Partial Critical/Legendary may damage weapon or partially defeat parry. Parrying after attacking requires Parry quality or a trait exception.

**Block (`L` or `R`):** Attack DC `13`, intercept hit. Shield = first wound free, remaining wounds convert to stamina loss; other objects = all wounds convert to stamina loss. Blocking items risk integrity loss. Bare arm = wounds to arm up to disabled mark, excess to intended target, no free mitigation.

**Cover/Concealment:** Stack with defence. Concealment = `+1 DC` ranged, melee only when awareness/movement meaningfully hindered, no damage absorption. Ranged cover = passive `+1 DC`, active (`F`) `+2 DC`, active benefit lost if moved out of position. Melee cover = interposed object (`L` or `R`) uses Block, structural cover (`F`) `+1 DC` without absorption. Total cover preventing access = no attack.

**Default Guard (`H`):** Automatic when aware, unengaged, `H` free; contest one approach. Committing `H` elsewhere ends Guard.

### Attacks

**Weapon use while grappling:** Only one-handed weapons may be used to attack while the wielder is in Grappling.

**Wild Swing (`L(R)`):** Weapon attack at `+2 DC`

**Focused Attack (`LH`):** Improved accuracy/control, careful strikes or called shots.

**Full Martial Attack (`L(R)HF`):** Fully committed, typically one advantage step or bonus damage, no Guard against additional approaches.

**Covering Fire (`LH`, ammo):** On success, defender chooses lose Guard until their next turn or suffer the attack; Partial Critical = both. Breaks shooter's Guard.

**Ranged attacks in close combat:** Firing from or into a Melee or Grappling engagement adds **+2 DC**. Apply this penalty once per attack, even if both shooter and target are engaged; other difficulty modifiers, including cover, stack normally. A shooter in Grappling may fire only a one-handed weapon.

**Attack damage and criticals:** A successful weapon attack deals its **listed base damage** from Equipment. On a **Partial Critical**, add **1 wound** and apply the weapon's Partial Critical property effect. On a **Legendary Success**, first apply those Partial Critical bonuses, then **double all numerical values of the attack's effects**, including wounds, armor Integrity loss, additional stamina loss, and armor conversion penalties. Double these effects before resolving normal damage mitigation. One-handed baseline damage and bow damage are **1/2/4 wounds**; two-handed baseline damage and pistol damage are **2/3/6 wounds**; rifle damage is **3/4/8 wounds**, for normal/Partial Critical/Legendary hits. Check each die for a Partial Critical against its own maximum, before flat bonuses. The attack must still meet or beat its DC; critical results do not turn a miss into a hit. Apply all wounds to the resolved hit location (or declared called-shot location), subject to normal damage mitigation. Critical damage does not add separate hits or allow retargeting between wounds.

**Example:** A two-handed axe deals **2 wounds** on a normal hit. A Partial Critical deals **3 wounds** and causes **2 armor Integrity loss** from Chopping. A Legendary doubles those values to **6 wounds** and **4 armor Integrity loss**.

**Hit locations:** Default hit = Core, Partial Critical hit = attacker's choice of arm/legs, Legendary hit (24+) = Head. Called shots declared before rolling: arm/legs unchanged DC, Head `+3 DC`. Core Partial Critical may hit belt/bandolier gear instead (`1d4` Small slots, `1d100` Tiny slots). Blind attacks/grapples typically `+4 DC`.

**Combat Awareness** (H, Default): as head action, you remain fully aware of all known combatants.  Without combat awareness you have difficulty attacking with melee and ranged weapons (DV +2) and defense (creatures attacking you have +1 advantage.)

### Random Hit Location

Use random hit location when an effect scatters, splashes, detonates, burns unpredictably, or otherwise does not target a single deliberate body region. See [combat.md](combat.md#random-hit-location).

Roll `1d12`:

|  Roll | Location    |
| ----: | ----------- |
|     1 | Head        |
|   2-5 | Core        |
|   6-7 | Right Arm   |
|   8-9 | Left Arm    |
| 10-11 | Legs        |
|    12 | Pack / Gear |

Pack / Gear is not a body wound track. It means carried or worn equipment is hit, damaged, ignited, contaminated, or otherwise affected as appropriate to the source.

For spreading effects, roll once for the first location, then keep rolling for additional affected locations until a roll repeats. Apply the effect to each unique location rolled before the repeat.

### Position and Readiness

**Advance (`F`):** Move one range closer (Separated, Melee, Grappling), typically Athletics against Guard. **Move (`F`):** Reposition/retreat/change facing, retreat safe unless surrounded/restrained, preserves Guard while `H` remains free.

### Interrupts and Follow-ups

**Triggered Action:** You may ready an action **before combat**, while actively waiting for a specified condition, or **on your turn during combat**. Declare the exact trigger, response, timing, required anchors, and ammunition/stamina costs. Commit at least `H` plus all anchors the action normally requires; those anchors are unavailable for defense while readied, and others can see you are waiting.

- **Interrupt:** Resolve when the trigger starts, before the triggering action fully resolves.
- **Follow-up:** Resolve when the trigger finishes, after the triggering action resolves.

Follow trigger wording exactly: "If something enters, I shoot it" includes any entrant; a narrower declaration triggers only on its specified condition. Once triggered, the response fires at its declared timing even if it is now undesirable.

Before combat, the declaration lasts while you maintain your attention and preparation. It carries through combat setup until the start of your first turn. During combat, it expires at the start of your next turn if untriggered; you may ready it again then. Resolve any triggered response at its declared timing, including when the trigger starts combat or occurs during setup movement. Apply normal action costs, checks, and current Fear restrictions, using the committed anchors, then continue setup or the existing turn order.

**Abort:** You may cancel before the trigger occurs. To abort a readied attack after it triggers, roll **`2d12`** against the normal attack DC. On a hit, choose **normal damage** or **no damage and a Minor Complication** instead; on a miss, the attack misses. Resolve the usual ammunition and stamina costs. See [Triggered Action](combat.md#triggered-action-spoken) for the broader combat rule.

## 4. Surviving the Consequences

### Wounds

A basic human has separate wound tracks for the **Head**, **Left Arm**, **Right Arm**, **Legs**, and **Torso (Core)**. Each track starts unwounded; each wound advances the affected location one step.

| Location     | Wound track                                                                                            |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Head         | injury(+1 DC) -> injury (+2 DC) -> Disabled -> Unconscious                                             |
| Left Arm     | injury(+1 DC) -> injury (+2 DC) -> Disabled                                                            |
| Right Arm    | injury(+1 DC) -> injury (+2 DC) -> Disabled                                                            |
| Legs         | injury(+1 DC) -> injury (+2 DC) -> Disabled                                                            |
| Torso (Core) | Injury (+1 DC) -> Injury (+2 DC) -> Injury (+3 DC) -> Injury (+4 DC) -> Injury (+5 DC) -> Unconscious |

Injury penalties increase the DC of actions involving the injured location. Use the current step's DC penalty and Bleeding rating; these replace earlier values on that location's track rather than adding to them. Disabled or Crippled arms and legs cannot be used. Crippled limbs remain attached and can recover through medical care.

**Final wound steps:** Reaching the fourth Head wound or sixth Core wound causes **Unconsciousness**; the character cannot act or defend. Reaching the final step of any wound track causes **Bleeding 3**. A track cannot advance beyond its final step: excess wounds cause that step's consequences rather than automatic death or dismemberment. Bleeding can still kill an unconscious character under the rules below.

### Stamina and Bleeding

A human typically starts with **3 stamina**, with a normal maximum of **3**. At **-5 stamina**, the character falls unconscious.

**Bleeding 1** causes the loss of **1 stamina per round**; **Bleeding 2** causes the loss of **2 stamina per round**; **Bleeding 3** causes the loss of **3 stamina per round**. Bleeding continues while a character is unconscious.

**Stop Bleeding:** Make a **Science check at DC 7 + 2 × the Bleeding value**: Bleed 1 is **DC 9**, Bleed 2 is **DC 11**, Bleed 3 is **DC 13**, and so on. Success removes the Bleeding condition entirely; on failure, bleeding continues normally. This stops further bleeding loss but does not heal wounds or restore lost stamina. Use the Treat Wound action (`L(R)H`) in [skills.md](skills.md#science).

If bleeding continues after unconsciousness, roll `1d6` once to determine the character's death threshold: **-5 - 1d6 stamina**. The character dies when bleeding reduces their stamina to or below that threshold. For example, a roll of `4` means death at **-9 stamina**.

Characters with **Diehard** roll `1d12` instead, giving a death threshold of **-5 - 1d12 stamina**.

**Guide suggestion:** Roll the death die in secret and keep the result and death threshold hidden, including for characters with Diehard. Players should not know exactly how many rounds a bleeding character has left to live.

### Healing and Medical Care

A night of rest with successful medical care reduces **each injured wound track by one level**, to a minimum of unwounded.

- One caregiver may treat **multiple patients in a night**. Choose the patients, spend **1 medical supply per patient**, and make one **Science check** for the night's care.
- **Treatment DC = 12 + total wound levels across all patients being treated + total festering days recorded on their wound tracks.** Include every wound being treated; treating more people increases the combined difficulty.
- Count each track's current step from unwounded: Injured is 1, Severe injury is 2, and so on. Each recorded day of festering adds another **+1 DC** for that track.
- **Example:** One patient has an arm at Severe injury (2) with 2 festering days; another has a Cut/bruised torso (1) with no festering. Treating both costs 2 medical supplies and requires **DC 17** (`12 + 2 + 1 + 2`).
- **Success:** After a night of rest, reduce each injured wound track of every treated patient by one level and prevent that night's festering loss.
- **Failure:** The medical supplies are consumed, no wounds recover, and eligible wounds fester normally that night. Failed medical attention provides no protection. The caregiver may push the roll using the normal Push Your Luck rules.
- **Wound-caused unconsciousness:** The character can wake once the Head is below 4 wounds, the Core is below 6 wounds, and stamina is above -5. Healing one cause does not remove another.

This treatment supports overnight recovery; use **Stop Bleeding** above for immediate first aid.

### Untreated Wounds

Wounds at **level 2 or higher** that receive no successful medical care fester. At the end of each night, lose **1 maximum stamina per qualifying wound track** and record **1 additional festering day on each such track**. Count each qualifying track once, not once per accumulated wound level. This includes the torso's second step, Injury (+1 DC).

Each festering day increases that wound's future healing DC contribution by **+1**. Successful medical care prevents another festering day that night; a failed attempt does not. A track below level 2 stops accumulating festering days, but retains its existing festering penalty until fully healed.

**Example:** An untreated Severe injury to an arm (level 2) and Disabled legs (level 3) reduce maximum stamina by **2 that night**. A typical human's maximum falls from 3 to 1. If both tracks remain untreated the following night, maximum stamina falls by another 2.

Track lost maximum stamina against the wound track that caused it. When that track **fully recovers to unwounded**, restore all maximum stamina lost to its festering and clear its festering days. Partial healing does not yet restore that maximum. Restoring maximum stamina restores capacity; recovery of current stamina is handled separately.

## 5. Magic

Certain spells and rituals can knit flesh, reattach limbs (even somebody else's), or potentially revive the dead. See the [ritual descriptions](Alhazred/rituals.md) for more information.

### Knit-Flesh

Through extraordinary concentration, the caster stitches living or dead flesh together. Choose how many wounds **W** to heal from one body location before casting. On a successful Magic check, reduce that location's wound track by W levels. If a limb has been severed in the fiction, it can be reattached, even if it belongs to somebody else, leaving the location at Disabled. The process is unholy and excruciatingly painful, causing **1 Fear** to a living recipient. Repairing dead tissue does not itself revive the dead. See [Knit-Flesh](Alhazred/rituals.md#knit-flesh).

- **Magic DC:** 12 + 2 × W
- **Stamina cost:** 3 × W
- **Backlash on failure:** Copy the W wounds you attempted to heal onto the matching body location on yourself.

### Reanimation

An intricate ritual that revives the dead on a successful Magic check, provided the body is a usable vessel. The revived character automatically gains a permanent madness (Bane) associated with their cause of death; see [madness and Banes](fear-and-initiative.md#mental-breaks-and-permanent-banes). A malevolent spirit always returns with them and co-occupies their body, creating a consequence that must be dealt with through the story. See [Reanimation](Alhazred/rituals.md#reanimation).

- **Magic DC:**  Same scene DC 15, same day DC 17, same week DC 19, same year DC 21.
- **Stamina cost:** 10
- **Backlash on failure:** The malecolent spirit takes full control of the charater, with the original soul helpless to do anything.

### Gout of Fire

Choose damage **D** before casting. The target takes D wounds regardless of the Magic check.

- **Magic DC:** 13 + D
- **Stamina cost:** 2 + D
- **Backlash on failure:** D wounds to the casting arm.

**Spillover (target and caster):** Start at a random body location for the target and the casting arm for backlash. Apply wounds until that location's track is full, then roll another location for remaining wounds. Stop when damage runs out or a previously rolled location repeats; discard any remaining wounds. The casting arm counts as already rolled.
