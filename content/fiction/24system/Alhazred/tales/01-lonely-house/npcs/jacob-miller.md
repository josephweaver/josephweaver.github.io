## **Jacob Miller** (The Handyman)

### Ideal Skills

* Melee, Athletics, Science

### Theme

Duty, loyalty, protection.

### Description

The Handyman has worked on the house for years. He knows every room. Every repair. Every addition. Every creaking floorboard. He speaks little. Works hard. And notices more than anyone realizes.

### Example Memories

* Repairing storm damage.
* Teaching Mr. J how to use a tool.
* Carrying supplies for the nursery.
* Seeing arguments nobody believed he witnessed.
* Watching over Ms. J when Mr. J was away.

### Possible Motives

* Broken promises.
* Loyalty to Ms. J.
* Anger at Mr. J's neglect.
* Protecting someone else.

---

**Jacob wants to keep the household safe and fulfill his promises.** He is a longtime handyman and caretaker, comfortable with practical problems and increasingly troubled by things he cannot repair.

His affection for Alice is platonic. His relationship with Mr. J includes genuine friendship, which makes the House’s accusation a meaningful betrayal.

| Aspect                                   | Jacob’s story                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What he initially wants**        | Maintain the house, finish the nursery, and earn an honest living among people he cares about.                                  |
| **What he eventually wants**       | Help Alice leave safely and persuade Mr. J that something is wrong with the house.                                              |
| **How the House welcomes him**     | Remembers his chair, warms the kitchen before his arrival, and makes him feel useful and expected.                              |
| **How the House keeps him coming** | Creates small failures: sticking doors, loose fittings, recurring leaks. Repairs provide reasons to return.                     |
| **How their relationship changes** | Jacob notices that repairs fail when someone prepares to leave. When he helps Alice pack, doors jam and her luggage disappears. |
| **What Jacob believes**            | The house behaves strangely, and Mr. J may know why. He has suspicions rather than a complete explanation.                      |
| **What the House believes**        | Jacob’s job is to preserve the household. Helping Alice leave violates that role.                                              |
| **His relationship with Alice**    | She trusts him and asks for practical help leaving. He promises it; neither requests nor promises violence.                     |
| **His relationship with Mr. J**    | Years of work, shared jokes, and mutual trust. Jacob can speak plainly to him and approach him without suspicion.               |

**His established pair is means and opportunity. Motive remains open.** Protecting Alice is a possibility the House suggests, rather than proof that Jacob considered Mr. J dangerous enough to remove.

> “You promised she would get out. What did you do when he stood in her way?”

The question quietly invents an obstacle: nothing establishes that Mr. J actually stood in Jacob’s way.

After the disappearance, Jacob remains in the caretaker’s cottage and occasionally tends the property. **He is the one person who keeps returning without needing the House to manufacture a reason.** That gives their relationship a sad irony: the House tries to compel companionship while failing to recognize the loyalty already offered to it.
