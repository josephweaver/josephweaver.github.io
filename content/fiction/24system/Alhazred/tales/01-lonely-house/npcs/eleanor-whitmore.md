## **Eleanor Whitmore** (The Business Partner)

### Ideal Skills

* Science, Social

### Theme

Independence and frustration.

### Description

A self-made businesswoman. Sharp minded. Practical. Capable. She helped build the business that made Mr. J wealthy. She respects him. She may even care about him. But she refuses to belong to him.

### Example Memories

* Reviewing accounts late into the night.
* Correcting one of Mr. J's mistakes.
* Discussing expansion opportunities.
* Rejecting unwanted romantic advances.
* Wondering why such a capable man wastes himself.

### Possible Motives

* Business disagreements.
* Betrayal.
* Resentment.
* A relationship that never became what Mr. J wanted.

---

**Eleanor wants to preserve the business and her independence.** She helped build the company, but increasingly believes Mr. J is using their partnership to pursue her romantically. His behavior threatens both her livelihood and the employees who depend on them.

| Aspect                               | Eleanor’s story                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What she initially wants**   | A dependable business partner who respects her judgment and boundaries.                                                                                                                                     |
| **What she eventually wants**  | Mr. J removed from directing the business, without allowing him to dismantle it or withdraw its resources.                                                                                                  |
| **How the House welcomes her** | Gives her a comfortable chair, remembers her tea, and makes her feel expected. It notices that Mr. J enjoys her company.                                                                                    |
| **How the House interferes**   | Misplaces contracts, withholds messages, and manufactures urgent business problems requiring her to stay overnight. It repeatedly arranges private encounters with Mr. J.                                   |
| **What Eleanor believes**      | Her married partner is engineering professional emergencies to keep her close. Rejecting him may put the company—and her own future—at risk.                                                              |
| **What the House believes**    | If Eleanor stays long enough, their affection will become romance, and she will join its household permanently.                                                                                             |
| **Her connection to Harrow**   | She handles his invoices, challenges his prices, and knows how to contact him. She considers him dishonest and detests dealing with him. Familiarity makes a conspiracy plausible without establishing one. |

Her motive has a practical constraint: **Mr. J must disappear in a way that leaves the company functioning.** Simply walking away from him would surrender what she helped build.

**Her established pair is motive and means. Opportunity remains open.** The players can invent a secret return, an advance arrangement, or somebody acting for her. The clues establish none of those.

The House’s accusation could be:

> “You went away. Victor stayed. You knew how to reach him.”

Historically, Eleanor departed without arranging Mr. J’s disappearance. She subsequently kept the company alive and made it successful. The House treats that success as confirmation:

> “You said it would prosper without him. And it did.”
