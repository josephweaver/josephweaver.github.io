## **Victor Harrow** (The Antiquarian)

### Ideal Skills

* Magic, Thievery 2

### Theme

Obsession and discovery.

### Description

Explorer. Scholar. Smuggler. Grave robber. The title depends upon who is speaking. The Antiquarian acquires rare things from forgotten places. Many of Mr. J's most valuable artifacts originated with him. Many of his most dangerous ones as well.

### Example Memories

* Excavating a forgotten tomb.
* Smuggling crates through customs.
* Discovering a relic no one should possess.
* Watching Mr. J keep an artifact for himself.
* Arguing over ownership of a remarkable find.

### Possible Motives

* Professional jealousy.
* Stolen profits.
* Hidden discoveries.
* Dangerous secrets.

---

**Victor Harrow wants to protect his livelihood, conceal his illicit dealings, and recover the Listener before anyone understands what he sold.** Mr. J’s disappearance could silence a dangerous witness—but it also leaves Harrow frightened of the artifact.

| Aspect                                       | Harrow’s story                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **What he initially wants**            | Profit from selling antiquities, with few questions about their origins.                                                                                                 |
| **What he eventually wants**           | Recover the Listener and prevent Mr. J from handing incriminating records to Blackwood.                                                                                  |
| **How the House welcomes him**         | Recognizes the man who brought it to Mr. J. His visits mean new objects, unfamiliar stories, and attention.                                                              |
| **How the House interacts with him**   | Agatha follows its hints and sends Harrow descriptions of her discoveries. Those reports reveal that the supposedly inert artifact has awakened.                         |
| **How the House obstructs him**        | Misplaces his packing materials, frustrates his searches, and conceals access to the artifact. It welcomes his visits but resists being taken away.                      |
| **What Harrow believes**               | Mr. J has awakened something dangerous. Blackwood’s investigation could expose both the stolen antiquities and Harrow’s responsibility for bringing the Listener here. |
| **What the House believes**            | Harrow wants to separate it from its friend and home.                                                                                                                    |
| **His secret arrangement with Agatha** | Help locate and sell the “hidden collection” without Mr. J’s permission. Agatha expects treasure; Harrow increasingly seeks one particular object.                    |
| **His connection to Eleanor**          | Years of disputed invoices and private correspondence. They know each other well enough to negotiate, threaten, or bargain; no arrangement against Mr. J is established. |

His motive needs a concrete deadline: **Mr. J will give Blackwood the original shipping records tomorrow.** Recovering the artifact alone would no longer protect him. Mr. J can identify the transactions and testify about them.

**His established pair is motive and opportunity. Means remain open.** His antiquarian expertise can suggest possibilities, but we should avoid giving him a proven disappearance spell, poison, or prepared weapon.

The House can offer competing accusations:

> “He was going to tell Thomas. You needed him quiet.”

Or, pointing toward Eleanor:

> “She promised you payment. What was the matter you settled?”

**Harrow’s actual secret is attempted theft and concealment.** He came to recover the Listener and persuade Mr. J to withhold the records. He did not make Mr. J disappear. Afterward, he fled because an unexplained disappearance made everything he already feared considerably worse.
