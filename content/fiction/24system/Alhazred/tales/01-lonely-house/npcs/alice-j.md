## Alice J (Ms. J)

### Associated Skills

* Social, Lore

### Theme

Love, loneliness, expectation.

### Description

A young woman who married Mr. J within the past few years. Whether she married for security, duty, affection, or desperation is unclear. She expected a family. She expected children. She expected a future. The house contains many signs of that future. Very few signs of its realization.

### Example Memories

* Standing in the unfinished nursery.
* Watching Mr. J leave on another business trip.
* Reading a book alone beside a window.
* Wondering whether Mr. J regrets the marriage.
* Seeing the Handyman carrying lumber and feeling unexpectedly safe.

### Possible Motives

* Jealousy.
* Rejection.
* Frustration.
* Fear of a childless future.

---

**Alice wants control over her own future.** She married Mr. J expecting companionship and a family. Now she believes he is keeping her in a marriage he no longer cares about—and preventing her from leaving.

Her desire for children explains what she hoped for. **Her apparent captivity supplies the stronger motive.**

| Aspect                               | Alice’s story                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What she initially wants**   | A present husband, children, and a home they build together.                                                                                                      |
| **What she eventually wants**  | To leave safely and reach relatives who will support her.                                                                                                         |
| **How the House welcomes her** | Warms her rooms, remembers her domestic routines, and encourages preparations for the nursery. Alice represents the lively family it hopes to have.               |
| **How the House interferes**   | Hides invitations and appeals for help, intercepts replies, misplaces travel necessities, and makes doors stick when she prepares to depart.                      |
| **What Alice believes**        | Mr. J controls the household and is deliberately cutting off her escape. His apparent pursuit of Eleanor makes that control especially cruel.                     |
| **What the House believes**    | Keeping Alice home gives the marriage time to become happy again.                                                                                                 |
| **How Jacob becomes involved** | Alice asks him to help her leave. He promises assistance. Their relationship is affectionate and platonic; whether that promise could mean violence remains open. |

The clues should move from  **disappointment → attempted departure → apparent confinement → possible conspiracy** .

| Clue                                                                                         | Whose?                    | Location                                 | How it implicates Alice                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Unfinished nursery plans and abandoned purchases.                                            | Alice and Mr. J           | Study drawer; nursery.                   | Establish the future she expected and lost. Background, rather than sufficient motive by itself. |
| Packed bag with clothes, money, and marriage certificate.                                    | Alice                     | Behind coats in Room 2.                  | Shows a practical intention to leave permanently.                                                |
| Pleas for help alongside unopened replies.                                                   | Alice and her relatives   | Concealed behind a linen-cupboard panel. | Supports her belief that somebody is isolating her. Nothing identifies who hid them.             |
| Recalled words: “I cannot stay here. Help me leave.” Jacob answers, “You have my word.”  | Alice and Jacob           | Kitchen.                                 | Suggests cooperation; leaves what she asked him to do unresolved.                                |
| A private conversation shortly before Mr. J vanished. She enters the study; the door closes. | The House’s recollection | Study doorway.                           | Establishes opportunity without supplying the conversation’s contents.                          |

**Her established pair is motive and opportunity.** She believes she cannot escape while Mr. J controls her life, and she had private access to him that evening. Her means remain completely open.

The House might suggest:

> “Jacob could have helped you. He always helped you.”

That is an accusation the players can develop, rather than evidence that Alice recruited him.

For the underlying truth, I would have  **Mr. J finally agree that Alice may leave during their last private conversation** . She leaves the study believing an escape is possible; afterward, he confronts the Listener. The House remembers her desperation and the closed door, but fails to understand that the conversation ended in agreement.

This gives Alice something meaningful to insist upon as her role emerges: **“I wanted to leave him. That does not mean I wanted him gone.”**
