## **Thomas Blackwood** (The Investigator)

### Ideal Skills

* Lore, Ranged

### Theme

Truth and suspicion.

### Description

An authentication agent, investigator, and expert in antiquities.

Employed by museums, collectors, and insurance firms.

His purpose is determining whether artifacts are genuine, stolen, forged, or dangerous.

He arrived believing Mr. J was hiding something.

### Example Memories

* Examining shipping manifests.
* Discovering missing provenance records.
* Following Mr. J across several states.
* Arguing over the authenticity of a relic.
* Finding evidence that should not exist.

### Possible Motives

* Professional disgrace.
* Fraud.
* Smuggling.
* Protecting a client.

---

**Blackwood has enough connections to belong in the mystery, and enough capability to be a suspect.** His distinctive role is that he correctly notices the interference but initially attributes it to people.

| Aspect                                    | Blackwood’s story                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **What he wants**                   | Establish the artifacts’ provenance, expose Harrow’s illicit trade, and understand the strange incidents surrounding the collection.                                                           |
| **Why he comes**                    | Agatha seeks an independent opinion after Harrow’s questions become suspiciously specific. Her correspondence connects the household’s disturbances to an artifact Blackwood has been tracing. |
| **How the House welcomes him**      | His questions feel like attention. It brings familiar objects and remembered scenes to his notice.                                                                                               |
| **How the House turns against him** | He recommends removing the Listener for examination. It begins misplacing notes, replaying misleading conversations, and suggesting Harrow has already taken it.                                 |
| **What Blackwood believes**         | Someone is obstructing his investigation and manipulating the household. He initially suspects Harrow, possibly assisted by someone inside.                                                      |
| **What the House believes**         | Blackwood intends to take its closest companion away—or take the House itself away from everyone.                                                                                               |
| **His relationship with Mr. J**     | Initial suspicion becomes cooperation. Mr. J agrees to supply records and privately show him the artifact.                                                                                       |

| Clue                                                                        | Whose?    | Location                | How it implicates Blackwood                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Letter requesting a second opinion about Harrow and the hidden collection.  | Agatha    | Blackwood’s notebook.  | Establishes his involvement in the household’s secret dealings.             |
| Revolver and cartridges.                                                    | Blackwood | Locked traveling trunk. | Gives him a direct means of coercion or killing.                             |
| Correspondence confirming access to discreet transport and secure storage.  | Blackwood | Traveling trunk.        | Gives him the resources to remove and confine someone.                       |

| Draft receipt for voluntary surrender of the artifact, unsigned.            | Blackwood | Study desk.             | Shows a planned removal the House could confuse with Mr. J’s disappearance. |
| Notes distinguishing observed events from witnesses’ interpretations.      | Blackwood | His room.               | Helps players discover that the House’s accusations exceed its evidence.    |

**His established pair is means and opportunity. His motive stays absent.** Keep evidence that Mr. J was cooperating: the players must invent why Blackwood would turn against a helpful witness.

The House’s accusation is simple:

> “You came to take something away. Then he was gone.”

One useful wrinkle: **Blackwood should have a correct insight the House refuses to accept.** His notebook might say:

> “Every account assumes an offender. None establishes a crime.”

That makes him consequential even before the players decide what to do with his role.
