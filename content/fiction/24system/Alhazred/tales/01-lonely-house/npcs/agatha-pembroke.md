## **Agatha Pembroke** (Aunt Agatha)

### Ideal Skills

* Social, Thievery

### Theme

Entitlement and greed.

### Description

Mr. J's elderly aunt. Sharp-tongued. Manipulative. Far more observant than she appears. She has spent years visiting the house. She is convinced a hidden fortune exists somewhere within its walls. No evidence has ever supported this belief. That has never discouraged her.

### Example Memories

* Tapping walls looking for hollow spaces.
* Searching desk drawers.
* Reading private correspondence.
* Rearranging furniture to inspect floorboards.
* Wondering why Mr. J never married someone more suitable.

### Possible Motives

* Inheritance.
* Family resentment.
* Financial desperation.
* Belief that Mr. J is hiding wealth.

---

**Agatha wants what she believes the family is owed.** As Mr. J’s aunt, she regards his wealth as a family fortune he has selfishly concealed. The treasure hunt and her expected inheritance are two routes to the same goal.

| Aspect                                  | Agatha’s story                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What she initially wants**      | Find the hidden fortune and establish her claim to it. She wants vindication as well as money.                                                                  |
| **What makes it urgent**          | She learns Mr. J intends to make a will leaving his collection to a museum. Her expected inheritance could disappear.                                           |
| **How the House welcomes her**    | Responds to her searches with hollow sounds, misplaced keys, loose boards, and glimpses of concealed passages.                                                  |
| **How the House manipulates her** | Offers enough discoveries to keep her returning, without allowing the search to reach a conclusion.                                                             |
| **What Agatha believes**          | Mr. J has hidden family property. The strange happenings suggest she is getting close—and perhaps that something wants her to find it.                         |
| **What the House believes**       | Agatha enjoys searching. Giving her another mystery is a way to keep her company.                                                                               |
| **Her arrangement with Harrow**   | She supplies sketches, inscriptions, and accounts of disturbances. He promises to help locate, value, and sell the collection without Mr. J’s permission.      |
| **Why she contacts Blackwood**    | Harrow becomes evasive about value and unusually interested in whether Mr. J speaks to the object. She suspects he plans to cheat her and seeks another expert. |
| **Her conflict with Mr. J**       | He discovers she has been sharing private information and demands that she stop. She believes he is protecting his hoard at her expense.                        |

**Her motive is threatened inheritance, sharpened by entitlement.** She believes she stands to gain substantially if he dies without changing his arrangements. Disappearance alone offers no immediate payout, so an inheritance-based accusation would require the players to explain how she expected his death to be established.

**Her established pair is motive and opportunity. Means remain open.** Harrow is a possible connection the players can develop; their existing agreement concerns property, without establishing a method of removing Mr. J.

The House might accuse her:

> “You said it ought to be yours. You knew he meant to give it away.”

Her later death completes the tragedy. She continues searching after Mr. J disappears, eventually reaching the concealed attic entrance and becoming pinned beneath the cabinet. **The House keeps encouraging her because it mistakes her obsession for happiness—and never recognizes when its faithful visitor needs help.**
