## Room 3: Parlor / Sitting Room

### At-a-Glance

* **Exits:** Double doors east to the Entrance Hall.
* **Features:** Large stone fireplace on the west wall, armchairs, sofa, occasional tables, books and magazines.
* **Items of Interest:** Family photographs, Eleanor's forgotten book, marks beside the fireplace, Aunt Agatha's tapping.
* **Purpose:** Establish the relationships among the household and introduce the House's tendency to remember familiar sounds.

### Read Aloud

> The double doors open into a comfortable sitting room dominated by a broad stone fireplace along the western wall. A small fire burns low in the grate, giving the room a warmth absent from the entrance hall.
>
> Several upholstered chairs and a sofa have been arranged around it, not formally, but as though chosen by people accustomed to spending long evenings together. Small tables hold books, newspapers, an abandoned pipe, and the remains of a half-played game of cards.
>
> Framed photographs occupy the mantel.
>
> Unlike the curiosities in the entrance hall, nothing here seems intended to impress a visitor. This is a room for reading, talking, arguing, and passing the evening while rain strikes the windows.
>
> For an empty house, it feels remarkably lived in.
>
> As you enter, there comes a faint sound from somewhere near the fireplace.
>
> *Tap.*
>
> Then silence.

---

### The Photographs

Several photographs stand upon the mantel.

Most show Mr. J with friends, business associates, or visitors to the house. His name is never written on any of them.

One photograph shows **Alice J.** standing beside him on the front steps shortly after their marriage. She appears young and genuinely happy.

Another shows **Eleanor Whitmore** and Mr. J standing beside an automobile outside a factory or warehouse. Eleanor is holding a rolled set of plans. Someone has written on the back:

> *Eleanor insisted it wouldn't work.*
>
> *She was right.*

A third photograph shows **Jacob Miller** repairing part of the porch while Mr. J watches. Jacob is looking directly at the camera with obvious annoyance.

These photographs establish that these people were not strangers brought together on a single disastrous night.

They had lives here.

---

### Clue: Eleanor's Book

A book rests beside one of the armchairs.

Inside the cover is written:

> **Eleanor Whitmore**

A railway timetable has been used as a bookmark.

Several passages have been underlined, but nothing about the book itself is particularly significant.

What matters is where it was left.

This was Eleanor's chair.

She spent enough evenings here to have a usual place beside the fire.

#### Memory Fragment

A fire burning late.

Mr. J speaking enthusiastically about some new venture.

Eleanor listening from this chair.

Alice across the room with needlework resting forgotten in her lap.

Nobody arguing.

Nobody saying anything improper.

Yet the room feels uncomfortable.

The player associates with **Eleanor Whitmore**.

---

### Clue: Marks Beside the Fireplace

A careful examination of the stonework reveals dozens of tiny chalk marks and scratches along the mortar.

Someone has tested the wall repeatedly.

Higher.

Lower.

Behind the mantel.

Beside the chimney breast.

A few locations have been marked with small crosses.

#### Memory Fragment

A walking cane tapping against stone.

A woman's voice:

> "Hollow."

Another tap.

> "No. Perhaps not."

Someone nearby sighs.

The woman ignores them and continues.

The player associates with **Aunt Agatha**.

---

### The Tapping

The tapping heard upon entering has no obvious source.

If the players investigate the fireplace, chimney, or surrounding wall, they find nothing unusual beyond Agatha's old search marks.

Do not emphasize the event.

The House is not attempting to frighten anyone.

It is remembering.

At irregular intervals during the investigation, the Guide may repeat:

> *Tap.*
>
> *Tap... tap.*

The sound should occasionally occur in other rooms as well.

Much later, the players may learn that Aunt Agatha spent years wandering the house and tapping upon its walls in search of Mr. J's supposed hidden fortune.

The House remembers the sound.

It misses her.

---

### House Behavior

Room 3 should be one of the safest-feeling rooms in Act I.

The fire remains warm.

Chairs are comfortable.

Books are where their owners left them.

If the players rest here, nothing attacks them.

The House wants them to sit.

To talk.

To tell stories.

To stay a little longer.
