## Room 6: Rear Hall

### At-a-Glance

The rear hall connects the front portion of the house with the Kitchen and other service areas. A back door leads outside. Just beyond it, a set of exterior cellar doors provides access to the Basement. The hall is narrow and practical, with hooks for work coats, a boot scraper, and signs of years of traffic between the house and grounds.

### Read Aloud

> The passage from the front of the house narrows into a plain service hall. Here the polished wood and decorative molding give way to painted walls, worn floorboards, and the practical fixtures of a working household.
>
> Several hooks line one wall. A battered work coat hangs from one of them beside a canvas tool bag. Mud has dried in the grooves of a heavy boot scraper near the rear door.
>
> The door itself is fitted with a broad window of rippled glass. Beyond it lies the back of the house and the darkening grounds.
>
> Through the glass you can just make out a pair of sloping cellar doors set against the foundation.
>
> Somewhere within the wall beside you comes a faint:
>
> *tap... tap.*
>
> A pause.
>
> *Tap.*
>
> Then nothing.

### The Back Door

The back door opens normally onto the grounds behind the house. The yard is less carefully maintained than the front, with a woodpile, an old chopping block, and a narrow path disappearing toward the trees.

Some distance beyond the house, the roof of a small cottage can be glimpsed through the branches.

This is **Jacob Miller's caretaker cottage**.

The path is sufficiently overgrown to suggest that nobody has walked it recently.

### The Cellar Doors

A pair of sloping wooden cellar doors rests against the foundation immediately outside the rear entrance. Heavy iron handles allow them to be pulled upward.

Beyond them, stone steps descend into darkness.

The steps lead directly into **Room 22: Coal / General Storage**.

The cellar is therefore completely separate from the main staircase. There is no interior stairway from the first floor into the Basement.

The doors are not locked.

They are simply heavy.

When opened, the players are greeted by the smell of cold stone, coal dust, and damp earth.

The Basement may be explored whenever the players choose.

### Clue: Jacob's Tool Bag

The canvas bag hanging beside the back door contains ordinary carpentry tools: a hammer, folding rule, chalk line, awl, nails, and several worn pencils.

Nothing is unusual about them.

A player associated with Jacob immediately recognizes the bag as his.

For a moment they remember standing in this hallway while Mr. J complains about a sticking upstairs door. Jacob listens patiently before saying:

> "It's not the door. House settled again."

Mr. J laughs.

> "You always blame the house."

Jacob does not.

### Agatha's Marks

Near the floor, several tiny pencil crosses have been drawn along the baseboard.

They continue down the hall.

One appears every few feet.

At each mark the plaster bears a small indentation.

Someone has been tapping along the wall and recording where the sound changed.

Most of the marks mean nothing.

One has been circled several times.

There is nothing behind it.

At least, not anymore.

### House Behavior

The Rear Hall is a useful place for the Guide to establish that the tapping is not confined to a single room.

If the players stop and listen after hearing it, another tap may answer from farther down the wall.

Then another.

For a moment it may almost seem to be moving away from them.

The House is not attempting to communicate.

It is remembering Aunt Agatha moving slowly through this hallway, tapping every few inches and listening carefully for the sound of an empty space.

The House remembers her search far better than it remembers what she was searching for.
