## Room 8: Pantry

### At-a-Glance

The pantry adjoins the Kitchen and contains dry goods, preserves, serving pieces, linens, and other household supplies. The room is narrow but meticulously organized. Alice's handwritten labels provide small glimpses of domestic life, while the condition of the food reveals an important limitation of the House: it remembers what things should look like, but cannot necessarily reproduce what they actually are.

### Read Aloud

> The pantry is narrow enough that two people would have difficulty passing between its floor-to-ceiling shelves. Tins, jars, bottles, serving dishes, candles, soap, and neatly folded linens fill nearly every available space.
>
> Everything has its place. Small handwritten labels divide the shelves into categories: **Baking**, **Breakfast**, **Preserves**, **Guests**, **Cleaning**, and **Cellar**.
>
> At first glance the pantry appears remarkably well stocked.
>
> Then you notice the smell.
>
> Something sweet beneath the dust. Something spoiled.
>
> An open jar of peaches sits upon one shelf. The fruit inside has collapsed into a dark mass beneath a layer of mold.
>
> Whatever keeps the stove warm does not seem capable of keeping food fresh.

### Alice's Labels

Most of the labels are written in the same hand as the household ledger in the Kitchen. The organization is practical rather than obsessive, with frequently used ingredients kept within easy reach and serving dishes or less common supplies stored above them.

One shelf bears a small handwritten label reading **Children**.

The shelf is empty.

There are no feeding dishes, bottles, or other supplies beneath it. Only the label remains. A player already associated with **Alice J.** recognizes the handwriting immediately. No memory fragment occurs.

### Clue: The Guest Shelf

The shelf marked **Guests** contains better coffee, expensive tea, additional candles, several unopened bottles, and tins of biscuits. Tucked beneath one tin is a handwritten note:

> Victor — coffee
> Eleanor — tea, no sugar
> Thomas — coffee
> Aunt Agatha — tea, far too much sugar
> Jacob — whatever is already made

The list is mundane and unexpectedly affectionate. These were not strangers assembled at the house on a single disastrous evening. They were regular visitors whose habits were familiar enough that the household prepared for them.

### Clue: Agatha's Tin

At the back of the Guest shelf sits an old biscuit tin containing several folded scraps of paper. They are covered with measurements and brief observations:

> Dining room chimney — solid.
> East wall of study — thinner than expected.
> Hall beside kitchen — hollow?
> Check behind pantry shelves.

One sentence has been underlined twice:

> **He has hidden it somewhere.**

Agatha apparently used the pantry to conceal notes from her searches. Examination of the walls reveals numerous tiny dents where she tested the plaster by tapping upon it. Despite her suspicions, there appears to be nothing hidden behind the pantry walls.

### The Preserves

Many of the sealed jars look perfectly normal until examined closely. Their contents have spoiled, sometimes far beyond anything that should remain recognizable. A few appear strangely fresh through the glass, but opening them immediately reveals the smell of decay.

The House remembers a pantry filled with food. It remembers the colors of the preserves, the arrangement of the jars, and Alice's labels. It does not understand what happened inside those jars as the years passed.

The House can reproduce the pattern.

It cannot reproduce the food.

### House Behavior

If the players remove something from the pantry and return later, they may discover another object occupying the empty space. It need not be the same object. A removed tin of coffee might be replaced by tea. A missing jar of preserves might be replaced by an empty jar bearing the appropriate label.

The House is not creating food. It is restoring the arrangement it remembers.

If the players deliberately empty an entire shelf and later return, they may find it neatly filled with empty tins, bottles, and jars.

Everything is properly arranged.

Everything is correctly labeled.

Everything is useless.
