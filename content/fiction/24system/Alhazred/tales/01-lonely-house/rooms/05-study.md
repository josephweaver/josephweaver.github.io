## Room 5: Study

### At-a-Glance

The Study is Mr. J's private working room and contains the densest collection of clues on the first floor. Tall bookcases, a large desk, a world map, and the fireplace reflect his interests in business, travel, and antiquities. Evidence found here connects him to Eleanor Whitmore, Victor Harrow, Alice, and Site 14 without yet revealing the true nature of the Listener.

### Read Aloud

> The room smells faintly of pipe tobacco, leather, and old paper. Tall bookcases line the walls from floor to ceiling, filled with travel journals, histories, atlases, scientific texts, and rows of carefully labeled account ledgers.
>
> A large desk occupies the center of the room. Its surface is surprisingly orderly: an ink bottle, several pens, a blotter, and a stack of correspondence tied neatly with ribbon. Behind it hangs a large map of the world marked with dozens of colored pins.
>
> A fire smolders weakly in the fireplace.
>
> For a moment, you have the distinct impression that someone was sitting behind the desk only moments before you entered.
>
> Then the sensation passes.

### Clue: The Shipping Ledger

One of the ledgers records shipments received by Mr. J over a period of several years. Most are mundane: machinery, textiles, books, furniture, and ordinary commercial goods. Mixed among them, however, are several entries recorded only by site designation.

> Site 14
> Site 22
> Site 31

Each is accompanied by dates, shipping expenses, and payments, but no description of what was transported.

A successful **Science or Lore** check reveals that the entries do not resemble ordinary commercial purchases. The unusual shipments were expensive, deliberately vague, and passed through several intermediaries before reaching Mr. J.

The player experiences a brief memory of dust and oppressive heat. Someone is prying open a wooden crate beneath moonlight. Inside rests something wrapped carefully in ancient cloth.

A voice says:

> "No museum can ever know about this."

The player associates with **Victor Harrow**.

### Clue: The Business Correspondence

The bundle of correspondence contains numerous letters exchanged between Mr. J and **Eleanor Whitmore**. Most concern ordinary business: contracts, expansion plans, expenses, employees, and investments. Eleanor's letters are direct and precise, and it quickly becomes apparent that she was not merely an employee. She routinely challenged Mr. J's decisions and, on several occasions, corrected his calculations.

Among the papers is an unfinished draft in Mr. J's handwriting:

> Eleanor,
>
> You are assuming expansion will solve the problem.
>
> Expansion is the problem.

The letter ends there.

A player examining the correspondence experiences a memory of working late into the evening. Ledgers cover the desk. Eleanor and Mr. J are arguing over figures. Neither is angry. Neither is willing to yield. There is affection beneath the disagreement, although its nature is difficult to determine.

The player associates with **Eleanor Whitmore**.

### Clue: Eleanor's Cigarette Case

Inside one of the desk drawers rests a silver cigarette case engraved with the initials:

> E.W.

Inside is a theater ticket stub dated approximately six months earlier.

There is nothing explicitly romantic about either object. Eleanor visited the house regularly and could simply have forgotten the case.

Nevertheless, Mr. J kept it.

A player examining the case remembers a railway platform in the rain. Eleanor adjusts her gloves before boarding. Mr. J stands beneath the station awning watching her leave.

The strongest feeling in the memory belongs to Eleanor.

Relief.

The player associates with **Eleanor Whitmore**.

### Clue: The Wall Map

The large map behind the desk contains dozens of pins marking ports, cities, archaeological sites, and remote regions throughout the world. Several correspond to locations appearing in Mr. J's shipping records.

One location is different.

The pin has been removed.

Only a small hole remains in the paper.

Beneath it, written faintly in pencil, are two words:

> Never Again.

A successful **Lore** check produces a brief memory: ancient stone beneath moonlight, the excitement of discovery giving way to the certainty that something should have remained buried.

The player associates with **Victor Harrow**.

The map does not identify the location as Site 14. That connection should require additional evidence.

### Clue: The Nursery Plans

A locked drawer in the desk contains personal documents rather than business records: household accounts, legal papers, Mr. J and Alice's marriage certificate, and several property documents.

Folded beneath them is an architectural sketch.

It depicts one of the upstairs rooms converted into a nursery. Measurements have been carefully written along the margins. Someone has sketched positions for a crib, dresser, rocking chair, and shelves.

The plans are approximately three years old.

A player examining them experiences a brief memory of standing over this very desk with Alice. She points at the drawing while Mr. J suggests moving the crib nearer the window. They discuss colors. Furniture. Names.

The conversation is filled with the casual certainty of people planning a future they assume will arrive.

It never did.

The player associates with **Alice J.**

### Clue: The Fireplace Ashes

The fireplace contains considerably more ash than the small remaining fire would suggest. Much of it consists of burned paper.

Most fragments crumble when touched, but careful examination recovers several partially legible pieces. Names and sentences have been destroyed beyond reconstruction.

One fragment contains only:

> "...if she discovers..."

Another bears what may be the bottom of a letter:

> "...cannot continue."

There is no signature.

A successful **Science** check confirms that these papers were deliberately burned rather than used simply to start the fire.

A player examining them experiences a sudden sensation of fear.

Not anger.

Not guilt.

Fear.

Someone stood before this fireplace destroying evidence because they desperately did not want another person to learn something.

The memory does not identify who burned the papers or who "she" was.

### House Behavior

The Study should feel different from the other rooms. The Parlor remembers companionship. The Dining Room remembers ritual. The Kitchen remembers hospitality.

The Study remembers **Mr. J**.

A chair may creak when no one is sitting in it. A page left on the desk may later be found neatly returned to the stack. The fire continues burning longer than it reasonably should.

But the House cannot reproduce Mr. J himself.

If a player sits behind the desk, the room seems almost comfortable for a moment.

As though something has returned to its proper place.

When the player stands to leave, the fire briefly dims.
