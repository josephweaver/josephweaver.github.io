## Room 1: Entrance Hall

### At-a-glance

* Exits: Front door to outside, Stairs to Floor 2, Passage to deeper inside, West/left double door to parlor, East/right double doors to dinning room, East door to closet.
* Features: Coat rack, Shoes and boots, Curio shelves.
* Items of Interest: old surveyor's rod, Needlepoint art work.

### Read Aloud

> Beyond the front door lies a broad entry hall paneled in dark oak. The room is illuminated only by the fading evening light filtering through narrow windows beside the door. Though the house appears empty, it does not feel abandoned.
>
> Directly ahead, a staircase rises to the second floor before dividing at a small landing. The banister bears the polished shine of long use, with some sort of art project resting in the crook at the banisters terminus ball.
>
> To your left, a pair of open double doors leads into what appears to be a sitting room. Beyond, you glimpse the outline of a large stone fireplace and several comfortable chairs gathered around it.
>
> To your right, another set of pocket doors stands partially open, revealing a dining room beyond. A long table occupies its center, shrouded in shadow.
>
> Near the stairs, a narrower passage disappears deeper into the house.
>
> A small door to the right of the front door stands slightly ajar.
>
> The left of the small door stands a brass coat rack holding several coats of differing styles and sizes. Beneath them rests an assortment of polished shoes and boots arranged neatly upon a mat. An umbrella stand contains three umbrellas, a walking cane, and what appears to be an old surveyor's rod.
>
> A curio of shelves along the west wall carry feature a variety of oddities: a brass astrolabe, a ship sealed within a bottle, a carved ivory elephant, and a black stone cat no larger than a man's hand. Nothing appears disturbed. Nothing appears recently used. And yet everything feels as though its owner may return at any moment.

### Clue: The Needlepoint Sampler

A framed piece of needlework hangs near the staircase. At first glance it appears decorative cloth with initials embroidered into the corner: `A.J.`.  The stitching is unfinished. A line of text abruptly stops midway through a phrase: `"Home is not where one..."`.

**Skill:** Lore 8 check completes the phrase. "Home is not where one lives, but where one is missed." and cause the player to remember a **Memory Fragment**. The player briefly experiences a sense of familiarity. A sunny room. A hoop of cloth. A woman working by a window. A feeling of waiting for someone to return.

The player associates with **Ms. J**.

### Surveyor's Rod

Leaning in the umbrella stand is a curious wooden pole nearly six feet in length. Brass fittings reinforce each section, allowing it to fold into shorter segments for travel. Faded measurement markings run along its length, though years of use have worn many of the numbers nearly smooth. The lower end bears scratches and small dents consistent with frequent use on stone and hard ground.

**Skill** Science 7 identifies Surveyor rod. The instrument appears practical rather than valuable, the sort of tool belonging to someone who spent long days measuring, building, or planning.

The player associates with **The Handyman**.

### Clue: The Locked Dispatch Box

A small brass lockbox sits among the curios. It is not hidden. Only locked.

**Skill:** Thievery 8 opens box.  Inside are several shipping tags. Most contain warehouse addresses. One contains coordinates written in shorthand notation and marked only: `Site 14`. No location is provided. **memory fragment**: The player recalls digging beneath moonlight. The excitement of uncovering something long buried. The certainty that the object belonged to no museum and no government.

The player associates with **The Antiquarian**.
