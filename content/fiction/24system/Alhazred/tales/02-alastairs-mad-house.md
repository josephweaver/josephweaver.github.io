
# Tale: Alastair's Mad House

> *The house is not haunted by a staircase. The staircase has been teaching the house how to become itself.*

## Premise

An old manor has become increasingly impossible to inhabit.

Guests suffer nightmares. Rooms no longer connect where they should. Doors lead to unexpected floors. The kitchen sometimes opens directly into the library. There appears to be no bathroom anywhere in the house except for a precarious washroom hanging from the third floor like an architectural afterthought.

At the center of everything stands a magnificent wrought-iron spiral staircase stretching from basement to attic.

The locals call the haunting **Alastair**.

They are wrong.

---

# The Guide's Truth

The staircase was installed during the early industrial age, when Fae still commonly crossed into the human world.

Its wrought-iron spiral accidentally created a powerful distortion in Fae magic.

Fae magic behaves as a projected field. Iron absorbs and redirects that field along its shape.

* Straight iron conducts.
* Bent iron redirects.
* Closed loops circulate or scatter.
* A sufficiently large spiral creates a recursive path.

The staircase became an accidental **Fae attractor**.

To Fae perception, its spiraling field resembled a road into the Far Realm.

It was not.

Fae entering the staircase became trapped within its circulating field.

Over decades, hundreds accumulated.

They were walked upon.

Children slid down their prison.

Servants scrubbed it.

Generations of humans climbed through them without ever knowing they were there.

Their identities gradually collapsed into one another until individual Fae ceased to exist.

Only one insane composite intelligence remained.

## The Stair

The Stair wants one thing:

**The destruction and abandonment of the house.**

If humans stop maintaining the structure, weather and decay will eventually break the iron spiral and release what remains of the trapped Fae.

Therefore The Stair does not initially seek to murder everyone.

It wants them to leave.

If terror fails, it escalates.

---

# Alastair

Historical records identify the entity haunting the house as **Alastair**.

Several sources use different spellings:

* Alastair
* Alastairs
* Alastairs
* àlastairs

The oldest surviving account was written by a French-speaking Alhazred agent.

Later archivists assumed **àlastairs** was a proper name.

It was not.

The original phrase was effectively:

**à la stairs**

The investigators have spent centuries looking for a being named Alastair.

There is no Alastair.

The true name is:

# STAIRS

---

# First Impression

The staircase dominates the center of the manor.

It is wide and aristocratic, constructed from black-lacquered wrought iron with deep cherry-wood steps. The railing twists into elaborate curls, knots, and repeating shapes that resemble Gaelic ornament or forgotten runes.

The patterns are not writing.

They are geometry.

The iron is always cold to the touch, carrying something like the temperature of the basement regardless of season.

The staircase rises continuously from cellar to attic.

It feels less like something installed inside the house than the structure around which the house was constructed.

---

# The House

The Stair has begun reorganizing the manor around itself.

It does not understand architecture in human terms.

It connects rooms according to association, memory, emotion, and Fae logic.

## Examples

* The **kitchen opens into the library** because both are places of consumption.
* The **nursery connects to the attic** because both contain forgotten things.
* The **dining room opens into the conservatory** because both represent cultivated abundance.
* The **master bedroom may open toward the cellar** because sleep resembles descent.
* A door may lead somewhere different when approached from the opposite direction.
* A hallway may become longer at night.
* Going upstairs may occasionally place someone on a lower floor.

The Stair is the axis of the manor.

As it twists, the house twists around it.

---

# The Bathroom Precipice

The house cannot tolerate bathrooms.

Bathrooms represent things fundamentally offensive to The Stair:

* bodily functions,
* waste,
* drainage,
* cleansing,
* running water,
* things leaving the house.

Attempts to install proper bathrooms inside the manor have repeatedly failed.

The only functioning washroom protrudes from the third floor.

It hangs beyond the exterior wall on aging timber and iron supports.

The room creaks.

It sways noticeably in strong wind.

The floor slopes slightly toward open air.

Its pipes hammer whenever water runs.

The bathroom did not feel built into the house.

**It feels nailed to it in protest.**

Ironically, the running water and its partial separation from the central structure make the Bathroom Precipice one of the places where The Stair's illusions are weakest.

It is magically safer.

It is architecturally terrifying.

---

# Manifestations

## Nightmares

Those sleeping in the house dream of:

* falling down endless stairs,
* climbing without reaching another floor,
* footsteps approaching from below,
* something enormous squeezing upward through the stairwell,
* standing at the top of the stairs while something calls from the cellar.

Repeated exposure causes sleepwalking.

Sleepwalkers tend to approach the staircase.

---

## False Tread

The Stair overlays Fae illusion onto physical hazards.

Examples:

* a missing step appears intact,
* rotten flooring appears sound,
* an open stairwell appears covered,
* a closed door appears open,
* a railing appears where none exists.

Physical interaction with iron may disrupt these illusions.

Passing an iron poker, nail, blade, or other iron object through a Fae illusion causes the false image to become translucent, scatter, or visibly distort around the iron.

---

## Banister Whisper

The Stair can transmit Fae Enchantment through its iron structure.

Victims may experience:

* intrusive thoughts,
* compulsions to climb,
* fragmented voices,
* sudden certainty that a particular room must be entered,
* unexplained impulses to open doors or windows.

Iron worn in a closed loop around the head strongly interferes with these signals.

Rather than receiving:

> OPEN THE CELLAR DOOR.

The protected victim may perceive:

> cellar... door... open... below...

The thought arrives scattered rather than actionable.

---

## Unfurl

When the house is dark and The Stair believes itself unobserved, portions of its iron structure can leave their architectural positions.

Banisters extend through corridors.

Black iron curls emerge from walls.

Rails twist along ceilings like vines.

Entire sections may creep silently through the manor before returning before dawn.

The lacquer remains pristine.

The wood does not move with it.

Only the iron unfurls.

---

## Housespin

The Stair can alter the apparent relationships among rooms.

The building does not physically rotate.

Instead, Fae spatial distortion uses the staircase as its central axis.

Characters may discover that:

* the kitchen now connects to the library,
* the library connects directly to the attic,
* a bedroom door opens onto a stair landing,
* the corridor they entered no longer exists,
* descending one floor returns them to where they began.

The stronger The Stair becomes, the less the manor obeys Euclidean expectation.

---

# Important Iron Facts

The Tale should reveal these progressively.

## First Iron Fact

**Iron interferes with Fae magic.**

Early folklore describes Fae as fearing "cold iron."

Temperature is irrelevant.

---

## Second Iron Fact

**Iron redirects Fae magic according to its shape.**

This is the critical investigative revelation.

Straight iron conducts.

Bent iron changes direction.

Closed iron may circulate or scatter.

Spiral iron may trap.

This fact should cause investigators to reinterpret earlier observations.

---

# The Crooked Painting

A murder or severe supernatural attack may occur in a bedroom no one could have entered.

The window was nailed shut from inside.

A painting above the bed hangs slightly crooked.

Neither detail is initially suspicious.

The window looks like an ordinary crude security measure in a dangerous world.

The crooked painting merely suggests careless maintenance.

After learning the **Second Iron Fact**, investigators may return.

The nail securing the window has been deliberately bent.

Its head aligns with the nail supporting the painting.

That nail is also bent.

Its head points toward the bed.

The attack never entered the room.

It was **routed into it**.

The mundane objects have not changed.

Their meaning has.

---

# Fae Iron Interaction

Fae magic is not itself Illusion, Enchantment, or another school.

It is a **means of projection** capable of carrying different effects.

### Examples

**Fae Illusion**
Iron disrupts the projected sensory information, producing translucence, doubling, scattering, or visual distortion.

**Fae Enchantment**
Iron scatters the projected thought, turning coherent commands into fragments, impressions, or emotional noise.

**Fae Projection**
Long iron objects may conduct an attack toward whatever touches the opposite end.

A rifle aimed directly at a Fae may therefore provide the Fae **Advantage** on an appropriate magical attack against its wielder.

The barrel acts as a conduit terminating near the shooter's hands and shoulder.

An iron circlet does nearly the opposite.

Its closed geometry scatters mind-directed Fae projection and substantially increases the wearer's DC against such effects.

# Investigation Structure

## Act I — The Mad House

The investigators enter expecting a haunting.

Present:

* the cold staircase,
* nightmares,
* strange room adjacencies,
* the Bathroom Precipice,
* the crooked painting,
* the nailed-shut window,
* inconsistent references to "Alastair."

Do not explain any of them.

---

## Act II — Iron

The investigators encounter evidence that iron disrupts Fae magic.

Traditional accounts refer to:

* horseshoes,
* bent nails,
* iron rings,
* iron over cradles,
* "cold iron."

Experiments reveal that temperature has little or no effect.

Geometry does.

---

## Act III — The Second Iron Fact

Investigators discover that iron does not simply repel Fae magic.

It redirects it.

A bent nail can turn a magical projection.

A ring can scatter it.

A spiral can trap it.

This should immediately allow attentive players to reconsider earlier clues.

---

## Act IV — Return to the House

The house is now understood differently.

The crooked painting implies a bent nail.

The crude window nail points somewhere.

The elaborate banister is no longer decorative.

Its curls are routing structures.

The staircase itself is a colossal magical apparatus.

The investigators realize:

**The haunting is not beneath the staircase.**

---

## Act V — Alastair

Records concerning "Alastair" become important.

Investigation reveals:

* the oldest document was written by a French agent,
* early copies use the unusual spelling **àlastairs**,
* later archivists normalized it into a person's name.

The investigators must recognize the linguistic mistake.

There is no Alastair.

There is only:

**STAIRS.**

---

# True Name

A true name is not necessarily a secret personal name.

It is a magically precise statement of what an entity **actually is**.

True-name magic therefore rewards correct ontology.

Calling the creature:

* spirit,
* ghost,
* Fae,
* Alastair,
* demon,

fails to precisely identify it.

Calling it:

# STAIRS

allows magic directed through its true name to target the collective entity.

A suitable exorcism might begin:

> By calling upon your true name, **STAIRS**, I cast you out.

---

# Possible Resolutions

## Destroy the Stair

Physically breaking the spiral releases the trapped Fae field.

This may destroy The Stair.

It may also release hundreds of fragmented Fae identities at once.

This is not necessarily the safest solution.

---

## Break the Geometry

The investigators interrupt key portions of the spiral with nonconductive material, remove sections, or alter critical bends.

The recursive field collapses without completely destroying the structure.

This may allow trapped identities to escape gradually.

---

## Reverse the Spiral

Characters who understand Fae-field geometry may attempt to turn the staircase from a trap into a route.

This could finally create the passage the original Fae believed they had found.

Success releases The Stair into the Far Realm.

Failure may attract additional Fae.

---

## True-Name Exorcism

The investigators identify **STAIRS** as the true name and conduct an exorcism capable of separating the entity from the physical staircase.

What emerges may not be one creature.

Hundreds of voices may suddenly remember themselves.

---

## Let the House Die

The Stair's own preferred solution.

Abandon the manor.

Stop repairs.

Allow water, rust, vegetation, and weather to destroy the structure naturally.

This may eventually release it.

Unfortunately, no one knows what happens when a century of insane accumulated Fae consciousness finally escapes.

---

# Guide Principles

### First ordinary, then ominous.

Important supernatural clues should initially appear to be ordinary environmental details.

### Player knowledge is evidence.

Players may freely use whatever folklore or game knowledge they possess.

Knowledge skills provide additional clues rather than permission to know something.

### Folklore remembers associations better than explanations.

Iron really matters to Fae.

"Cold iron" is simply the wrong explanation.

### Let simple rules create complex consequences.

Do not create a unique magical rule when an established interaction can explain the event.

### The mundane can defeat the supernatural.

A nail, circlet, bucket of water, or architectural observation may matter more than a spell if the players understand what they are dealing with.

---

# Central Reveal

The investigators begin by asking:

> What lives beneath the stairs?

They eventually learn they have been asking the wrong question.

> **What if the stairs are alive?**
