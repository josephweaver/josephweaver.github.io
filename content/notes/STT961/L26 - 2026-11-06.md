### General Donsker Theorem

Refinements of conditions in Donsker theorems:

1. Majorizing measure.
2. A Gaussian hypothesis.

There exist partitions

$$
\mathcal F=\bigcup_j\mathcal F_{\varepsilon j}^{n}
$$

as before, with

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E^\star\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal F_{\varepsilon j}^{n}}
|Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)|^2\leq\varepsilon^2.
$$

There exist discrete probability measures $\mu_n$ on $\mathcal F$ such that

$$
\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\int_0^{\delta_n}
\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\mu_n(\mathcal F_{\varepsilon}^{n}f)}}\,d\varepsilon
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0,
$$

where $\mathcal F_{\varepsilon}^{n}f$ is the partitioning set at level $\varepsilon$ to which $f$ belongs.

# 2. Gaussian and Gaussian Dominated Semimetrics - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.2 and Problem 2.11.4

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Definition.** A semimetric $\rho$ is called Gaussian if it is the standard deviation semimetric of a tight, zero-mean Gaussian random element $G$ in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$:

$$
\rho(f,g)=\sqrt{E\bigl(G(f)-G(g)\bigr)^2}.
$$

**Definition.** A semimetric $\rho$ is Gaussian dominated if it is bounded above on $\mathcal F\times\mathcal F$ by a Gaussian semimetric.

**Proposition.** Any semimetric $\rho$ such that

$$
\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,\rho)}\,d\varepsilon<\infty
$$

is Gaussian dominated.

Problems 2.11.1–2.11.4.

# 3. Bracketing by Gaussian Hypotheses - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.2, Theorem 2.11.11

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.** For every $n$, let $Z_{n1},\ldots,Z_{nm_n}$ be independent stochastic processes indexed by $\mathcal F$. Suppose there exists a Gaussian-dominated semimetric $\rho$ on $\mathcal F$ such that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E^\star\left\{
\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}
I\bigl(\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}>\eta\bigr)
\right\}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\eta>0,
$$

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^2
\leq\rho^2(f,g)
\qquad\text{for every }f,g\in\mathcal F,
$$

and

$$
\sup_{t>0}\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}t^2P^\star\left(
\sup_{f,g\in B(\varepsilon)}|Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)|>t
\right)\leq\varepsilon^2
$$

for every $\rho$-ball $B(\varepsilon)\subset\mathcal F$ of radius less than $\varepsilon$, and every $n$.

*Textbook clarification: The handwritten radius bound is written as “$\leq\varepsilon$”; Theorem 2.11.11 uses radius less than $\varepsilon$. The strict version above is the stated textbook hypothesis. See pp. 212–213.*

Then the sequence

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}(Z_{ni}-EZ_{ni})
$$

is asymptotically tight in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$. It converges weakly provided it converges marginally.

# 4. A Donsker Corollary Using a Gaussian Semimetric - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.2, Corollary 2.11.12

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Corollary.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a pre-Gaussian class of measurable functions whose envelope function possesses a weak second moment. Suppose there exists a Gaussian-dominated semimetric $\rho$ on $\mathcal F$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\left\|\sup_{f,g\in B(\varepsilon)}|f-g|\right\|_{P,2,\infty}
\leq\varepsilon
$$

for every $\rho$-ball $B(\varepsilon)\subset\mathcal F$ of radius $\varepsilon$.

Then $\mathcal F$ is $P$-Donsker.

# 5. Weighted Empirical Distribution Functions - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.2, Example 2.11.15

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Example.** Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n,\ldots$ be i.i.d. random variables with uniform distribution $P$ on $[0,1]$.

For a fixed function

$$
q:(0,1/2]\longrightarrow\mathbb R^+,
$$

consider the class of functions

$$
\mathcal F=
\left\{\frac{I_{(0,t]}}{q(t)}:0<t\leq\frac12\right\}.
$$

Assume $1/q$ is decreasing. Then $\mathbb G_n$ on $\mathcal F$ is a weighted empirical process.

The following statements are equivalent:

**(1)** $\mathcal F$ is Donsker.

**(2)** $\mathcal F$ is pre-Gaussian and

$$
\frac1{q(t)}=o\left(\frac1{\sqrt t}\right)
\qquad\text{as }t\downarrow0.
$$

**(3)**

$$
\int_0^{1/2}\frac1t
\exp\left\{-\frac{\varepsilon q^2(t)}t\right\}\,dt<\infty
\qquad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
$$

# 7. Uniform and Bracketing Entropy Conditions for Changing Classes - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.3, Theorems 2.11.22 and 2.11.23

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.** For every $n$, let

$$
\mathcal F_n=\{f_{n,t}:t\in T\}
$$

be a class of measurable functions indexed by a totally bounded semimetric space $(T,\rho)$ such that the classes

$$
\mathcal F_{n,\delta}
=\{f_{n,s}-f_{n,t}:\rho(s,t)<\delta\}
$$

and $\mathcal F_{n,\delta}^2$ are $P$-measurable for every $\delta>0$.

Suppose the envelope conditions and $(*)$ above hold, and

$$
\sup_Q\int_0^{\delta_n}
\sqrt{\log N\bigl(\varepsilon\|F_n\|_{L_2(Q)},
\mathcal F_n,L_2(Q)\bigr)}\,d\varepsilon
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0.
\tag{**}
$$

The supremum is over discrete probability measures $Q$.

Then $\{\mathbb G_n f_{n,t}:t\in T\}$ is asymptotically tight in $\ell^\infty(T)$ and converges to a Gaussian process provided the sequence of covariance functions

$$
P(f_{n,s}f_{n,t})-Pf_{n,s}\,Pf_{n,t}
$$

converges pointwise on $T\times T$.

*Correction: The first term in the handwritten covariance formula appears as $P(f_{n,s}-f_{n,t})$. Covariance requires the product $P(f_{n,s}f_{n,t})$, as in Theorem 2.11.22, p. 221.*

**Theorem.** Replace the uniform entropy condition by

$$
\int_0^{\delta_n}
\sqrt{\log N_{[]}\bigl(\varepsilon\|F_n\|_{L_2(P)},
\mathcal F_n,L_2(P)\bigr)}\,d\varepsilon
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0.
\tag{***}
$$

Then the same tightness and convergence conclusion holds.

*Textbook clarification: The last handwritten page uses ditto marks for the theorem setup and conclusion. Theorem 2.11.23, p. 221, requires a class of measurable functions indexed by the totally bounded $(T,\rho)$, the three conditions (2.11.21), and $(***)$. Unlike Theorem 2.11.22, it does not require the extra $P$-measurability of the difference and squared-difference classes. Its Gaussian limit is tight, and convergence again requires pointwise convergence of the displayed covariance functions.*
