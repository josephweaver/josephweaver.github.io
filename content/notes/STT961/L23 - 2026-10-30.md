# 1. Subclasses, Closures, and Symmetric Convex Hulls - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.1, Theorems 2.10.1-2.10.3, pp. 190-191

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.10. Permanence of the Donsker Property

**Definition.** Given a class $\mathcal F$ of measurable functions, let $\overline{\mathcal F}$ denote the set of all functions $f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R$ for which there exists a sequence $f_m\in\mathcal F$ such that

$$
f_m\longrightarrow f\qquad\text{pointwise and in }L_2(P).
$$

**Definition.** Let $\operatorname{sconv}\mathcal F$ denote the set of combinations

$$
\sum_{i=1}^\infty\lambda_i f_i,
\qquad f_i\in\mathcal F,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^\infty|\lambda_i|\le1,
$$

where the series converges pointwise and in $L_2(P)$.

*Textbook clarification: This is the symmetric convex hull, with possibly negative coefficients and total absolute coefficient sum at most one. The notes call these "convex combinations," as does the introductory definition in the textbook; the proof identifies the class as the symmetric convex hull. Pointwise convergence here means convergence at every argument, not merely almost surely (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.10.1, pp. 190-191).*

**Theorems.**

If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker and $\mathcal G\subset\mathcal F$, then $\mathcal G$ is Donsker.

If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker, then $\overline{\mathcal F}$ is Donsker.

If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker, then $\operatorname{sconv}\mathcal F$ is Donsker.

**Idea of proof.** Check:

1. Marginal convergence.
2. Asymptotic equicontinuity.

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The purpose of these permanence results is to avoid proving a new Donsker theorem every time a known class is modified. The professor connected the closure and symmetric-convex-hull constructions with basis expansions in functional data analysis: one may establish the property for basic functions and then pass to a larger class through the stated coefficient and convergence restrictions.

No full proof was given. The professor outlined the familiar two tasks of checking finite-dimensional convergence and asymptotic equicontinuity, explaining that the construction of the larger class allows control for the original class to be carried over.

# 2. Lipschitz Transformations of Donsker Classes - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.2, Theorem 2.10.6, p. 192

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Definition.** For given classes $\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k$ of real-valued functions defined on $\mathcal X$ and a fixed map

$$
\varphi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R,
$$

let $\varphi\circ(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k)$ denote the class of functions

$$
x\longmapsto\varphi\bigl(f_1(x),f_2(x),\ldots,f_k(x)\bigr)
$$

as $(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ ranges over $\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

**Theorem.** Let $\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k$ be Donsker classes with

$$
\sup_{f\in\mathcal F_i}\left|\int f\,dP\right|
=\|P\|_{\mathcal F_i}<\infty
\qquad\text{for every }i.
$$

Let $\varphi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R$ satisfy

$$
\left|(\varphi\circ f)(x)-(\varphi\circ g)(x)\right|^2
\le\sum_{\ell=1}^k\bigl(f_\ell(x)-g_\ell(x)\bigr)^2
$$

for all $x\in\mathcal X$ and $f,g\in\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

Then $\varphi\circ(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k)$ is Donsker, provided that

$$
\varphi\circ(f_1,\ldots,f_k)
$$

is square integrable for at least one $(f_1,\ldots,f_k)\in\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The next question is how to combine several Donsker classes, rather than modify only one. The professor emphasized that each coordinate function is chosen from its corresponding class before the map $\varphi$ is applied. The classes need not be different; several coordinates can use the same initial class.

The square-integrability requirement is imposed on only one transformed tuple. The professor explained that the displayed Lipschitz bound then lets one deduce square integrability for the other members. The theorem was stated without proof and followed by concrete operations to illustrate its use.

# 3. Examples of Operations Preserving the Donsker Property - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.2, Examples 2.10.7-2.10.11, pp. 192-193

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**1.** If $\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2$ are Donsker and

$$
\|P\|_{\mathcal F_1\cup\mathcal F_2}<\infty,
$$

then

$$
\inf(\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2),\qquad
\sup(\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2),\qquad
\mathcal F_1+\mathcal F_2,\qquad
\mathcal F_1\cup\mathcal F_2
$$

are Donsker.

**2.** If $\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2$ are uniformly bounded Donsker classes, then

$$
\mathcal F_1\mathcal F_2
$$

is Donsker.

**3.** If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker with $\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty$, and every $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfies $f\ge\delta>0$, then

$$
\left\{\frac1f:f\in\mathcal F\right\}
$$

is Donsker.

**4.** If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker with $\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty$ and $g$ is a uniformly bounded measurable function, then

$$
g\mathcal F=\{gf:f\in\mathcal F\}
$$

is Donsker.

**5.** If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker with integrable envelope $F$, then

$$
\mathcal F_M
=\{fI(F\le M'):f\in\mathcal F,\ M'\le M\}
$$

is Donsker.

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The examples show why different operations require different qualifications. The professor singled out the uniform bound for products and the lower bound away from zero for reciprocals, mentioning a suitably bounded-away-from-zero class of densities as a possible setting for the latter. Multiplication by one fixed bounded measurable function was described as especially simple, while truncation was emphasized as a useful practical operation.

# 4. Nonuniform Lipschitz Transformations - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.2, Corollary 2.10.13, p. 193

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Corollary.** Let $\varphi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R$ satisfy

$$
\left|(\varphi\circ f)(x)-(\varphi\circ g)(x)\right|^2
\le\sum_{\ell=1}^k L_{\alpha,\ell}^2(x)
\bigl(f_\ell(x)-g_\ell(x)\bigr)^2
$$

for given measurable functions $L_{\alpha,\ell}$, all $x\in\mathcal X$, and all $f,g\in\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

Let each class $L_{\alpha,i}\mathcal F_i$ be Donsker with

$$
\|P\|_{L_{\alpha,i}\mathcal F_i}<\infty.
$$

Then $\varphi\circ(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k)$ is Donsker, provided $\varphi\circ(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ is square integrable for at least one $(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$.

*Textbook clarification: The final condition in my notes is abbreviated by a continuation mark. Corollary 2.10.13 retains the square-integrability condition for at least one transformed tuple, just as in Theorem 2.10.6 (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.10.2, p. 193).*

**Idea of proof.** Without loss of generality, $L_{\alpha,\ell}>0$. Define

$$
\psi(f)
=\varphi\left(\frac{f_1}{L_{\alpha,1}},\ldots,
\frac{f_k}{L_{\alpha,k}}\right).
$$

$\psi$ satisfies the conditions of the theorem.

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The corollary allows coordinate-dependent weight functions in the Lipschitz bound. Its proof sketch changes variables to reduce the weighted condition to the preceding theorem, rather than developing a new convergence argument.

The professor emphasized how these results can be combined: start with tractable classes, form permitted expansions, and then combine coordinates through a transformation. The advantage is that one may not need to compute covering numbers, entropies, or VC dimensions for the complicated final class directly. The hard work can instead be concentrated on the initial classes. This strategy may solve only part of a problem if those initial classes are themselves difficult.

Questions about linear predictors and neural networks prompted further discussion of this building-block approach. The professor connected suitable VC properties with uniform limit results and contrasted the consistency associated with a Glivenko-Cantelli class with the square-root sample-size scaling in the empirical-process central limit theorem. These comments concerned classes meeting the relevant Donsker conditions, rather than an assertion that every unrestricted predictor or neural-network class has the property.

# 5. Independent Stochastic Processes on a Product Space - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, p. 205

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.11. CLT for Processes

For every $n$, let $Z_{n1},\ldots,Z_{n,m_n}$ be independent stochastic processes indexed by a common arbitrary semimetric space $(\mathcal F,\rho)$.

We are trying to generalize from

$$
Z_{ni}(f)=\frac1{\sqrt n}f(X_i).
$$

Independence is understood in the following way. We work on a product probability space

$$
\prod_{i=1}^{m_n}(\mathcal X_{ni},\mathcal A_{ni},P_{ni}).
$$

Each

$$
Z_{ni}(f)=Z_{ni}(f,x)
$$

depends only on the $i$th coordinate of $x$.

*Textbook clarification: These stochastic processes have bounded sample paths, so each can be regarded as a map into $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$ (van der Vaart & Wellner, introduction to §2.11, p. 205).*

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

Chapter 2.11 moves beyond the particular summands used in an ordinary empirical process. The professor explained that both the processes and their distributions may depend on the row index $n$, and the summands need not be identically distributed. The product-space description makes precise which coordinate supplies the randomness for each process.

The motivation again comes from the bootstrap. Its added random mechanism can change with sample size, whereas the multiplier results just discussed start with a fixed i.i.d. sequence of multipliers. The professor said that more general results would be needed for such applications, including settings in which the relevant function classes vary with $n$. The present setup begins that extension by studying sums of independent processes.

# 6. Central Limit Theorem under a Random Entropy Condition - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, Theorem 2.11.1, pp. 205-206

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.** For every $n$, let $Z_{n1},\ldots,Z_{n,m_n}$ be independent stochastic processes indexed by a totally bounded semimetric space $(\mathcal F,\rho)$.

Assume that the sums

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}e_iZ_{ni},\qquad e_i\in\{-1,0,1\},
$$

are measurable as indicated below, and that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}
E^\star\left[
\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}^2
I\bigl(\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}>\eta\bigr)
\right]
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\eta>0
$$

(the Lindeberg condition),

$$
\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E\bigl[Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr]^2
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0,
$$

and

$$
\int_0^{\delta_n}\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,d_n)}\,d\varepsilon
\xrightarrow{P^\star }0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0,
$$

where

$$
d_n^2(f,g)
=\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^2.
$$

Then the sequence

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}(Z_{ni}-EZ_{ni})
$$

is asymptotically $\rho$-equicontinuous. It converges in distribution in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$ provided the sequence of covariance functions converges pointwise on $\mathcal F\times\mathcal F$.

**Remark (measurability).** The maps

$$
(x_1,\ldots,x_{m_n})\longmapsto
\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}e_i\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^p\right|,
\qquad p=1,2,
$$

are measurable for every $\delta>0$, every $(e_1,\ldots,e_{m_n})\in\{-1,0,1\}^{m_n}$, and every $n$.

*Textbook clarification: "Measurable" in the theorem means the two scalar supremum maps above, not an unstated Borel measurability assumption on the entire $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$-valued sum. The handwritten remark is cut off at its lower edge; the powers $p=1,2$ and the quantifiers are made explicit from the exact assumption preceding Theorem 2.11.1. Measurability for the completed product probability spaces suffices (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.11.1, p. 205).*

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The professor identified the first condition as a process-level version of the Lindeberg condition. The other conditions control small increments and the entropy integral for the random semimetric built from the processes.

The measurability remark specifies the suprema that will actually occur in the proof, for powers one and two. The professor stressed that the placement of expectation matters and that an outer expectation cannot simply be inserted anywhere in an argument as a substitute for the needed measurability.

The proof was deferred to the next lecture. It was described as following the broad structure of the Donsker-theorem proof, but with the empirical-process summands replaced by the more general $Z_{ni}$, requiring the steps to be checked in that setting.
