# 1. Subclasses, Closures, and Symmetric Convex Hulls - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.1, Theorems 2.10.1-2.10.3, pp. 190-191

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.10. Permanence of the Donsker Property

**Definition.** Given a class $\mathcal F$ of measurable functions, let $\overline{\mathcal F}$ denote the set of all functions $f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R$ for which there exists a sequence $f_m\in\mathcal F$ such that

$$
f_m\longrightarrow f\qquad\text{pointwise and in }L_2(P).
$$

**Definition.** Let $\operatorname{sconv}\mathcal F$ denote the set of combinations

$$
\sum_{i=1}^\infty\lambda_i f_i,
\qquad f_i\in\mathcal F,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^\infty|\lambda_i|\le1,
$$

where the series converges pointwise and in $L_2(P)$.

**Theorems.**

If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker and $\mathcal G\subset\mathcal F$, then $\mathcal G$ is Donsker.

If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker, then $\overline{\mathcal F}$ is Donsker.

If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker, then $\operatorname{sconv}\mathcal F$ is Donsker.

**Idea of proof.** Check:

1. Marginal convergence.
2. Asymptotic equicontinuity.

# 2. Lipschitz Transformations of Donsker Classes - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.2, Theorem 2.10.6, p. 192

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Definition.** For given classes $\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k$ of real-valued functions defined on $\mathcal X$ and a fixed map

$$
\varphi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R,
$$

let $\varphi\circ(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k)$ denote the class of functions

$$
x\longmapsto\varphi\bigl(f_1(x),f_2(x),\ldots,f_k(x)\bigr)
$$

as $(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ ranges over $\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

**Theorem.** Let $\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k$ be Donsker classes with

$$
\sup_{f\in\mathcal F_i}\left|\int f\,dP\right|
=\|P\|_{\mathcal F_i}<\infty
\qquad\text{for every }i.
$$

Let $\varphi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R$ satisfy

$$
\left|(\varphi\circ f)(x)-(\varphi\circ g)(x)\right|^2
\le\sum_{\ell=1}^k\bigl(f_\ell(x)-g_\ell(x)\bigr)^2
$$

for all $x\in\mathcal X$ and $f,g\in\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

Then $\varphi\circ(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k)$ is Donsker, provided that

$$
\varphi\circ(f_1,\ldots,f_k)
$$

is square integrable for at least one $(f_1,\ldots,f_k)\in\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

# 3. Examples of Operations Preserving the Donsker Property - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.2, Examples 2.10.7-2.10.11, pp. 192-193

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**1.** If $\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2$ are Donsker and

$$
\|P\|_{\mathcal F_1\cup\mathcal F_2}<\infty,
$$

then

$$
\inf(\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2),\qquad
\sup(\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2),\qquad
\mathcal F_1+\mathcal F_2,\qquad
\mathcal F_1\cup\mathcal F_2
$$

are Donsker.

**2.** If $\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2$ are uniformly bounded Donsker classes, then

$$
\mathcal F_1\mathcal F_2
$$

is Donsker.

**3.** If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker with $\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty$, and every $f\in\mathcal F$ satisfies $f\ge\delta>0$, then

$$
\left\{\frac1f:f\in\mathcal F\right\}
$$

is Donsker.

**4.** If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker with $\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty$ and $g$ is a uniformly bounded measurable function, then

$$
g\mathcal F=\{gf:f\in\mathcal F\}
$$

is Donsker.

**5.** If $\mathcal F$ is Donsker with integrable envelope $F$, then

$$
\mathcal F_M
=\{fI(F\le M'):f\in\mathcal F,\ M'\le M\}
$$

is Donsker.

# 4. Nonuniform Lipschitz Transformations - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.10.2, Corollary 2.10.13, p. 193

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Corollary.** Let $\varphi:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R$ satisfy

$$
\left|(\varphi\circ f)(x)-(\varphi\circ g)(x)\right|^2
\le\sum_{\ell=1}^k L_{\alpha,\ell}^2(x)
\bigl(f_\ell(x)-g_\ell(x)\bigr)^2
$$

for given measurable functions $L_{\alpha,\ell}$, all $x\in\mathcal X$, and all $f,g\in\mathcal F_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal F_k$.

Let each class $L_{\alpha,i}\mathcal F_i$ be Donsker with

$$
\|P\|_{L_{\alpha,i}\mathcal F_i}<\infty.
$$

Then $\varphi\circ(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_k)$ is Donsker, provided $\varphi\circ(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ is square integrable for at least one $(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$.

**Idea of proof.** Without loss of generality, $L_{\alpha,\ell}>0$. Define

$$
\psi(f)
=\varphi\left(\frac{f_1}{L_{\alpha,1}},\ldots,
\frac{f_k}{L_{\alpha,k}}\right).
$$

$\psi$ satisfies the conditions of the theorem.

# 5. Independent Stochastic Processes on a Product Space - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, p. 205

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.11. CLT for Processes

For every $n$, let $Z_{n1},\ldots,Z_{n,m_n}$ be independent stochastic processes indexed by a common arbitrary semimetric space $(\mathcal F,\rho)$.

We are trying to generalize from

$$
Z_{ni}(f)=\frac1{\sqrt n}f(X_i).
$$

Independence is understood in the following way. We work on a product probability space

$$
\prod_{i=1}^{m_n}(\mathcal X_{ni},\mathcal A_{ni},P_{ni}).
$$

Each

$$
Z_{ni}(f)=Z_{ni}(f,x)
$$

depends only on the $i$th coordinate of $x$.

# 6. Central Limit Theorem under a Random Entropy Condition - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, Theorem 2.11.1, pp. 205-206

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.** For every $n$, let $Z_{n1},\ldots,Z_{n,m_n}$ be independent stochastic processes indexed by a totally bounded semimetric space $(\mathcal F,\rho)$.

Assume that the sums

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}e_iZ_{ni},\qquad e_i\in\{-1,0,1\},
$$

are measurable as indicated below, and that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}
E^\star\left[
\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}^2
I\bigl(\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}>\eta\bigr)
\right]
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\eta>0
$$

(the Lindeberg condition),

$$
\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E\bigl[Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr]^2
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0,
$$

and

$$
\int_0^{\delta_n}\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,d_n)}\,d\varepsilon
\xrightarrow{P^\star }0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0,
$$

where

$$
d_n^2(f,g)
=\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^2.
$$

Then the sequence

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}(Z_{ni}-EZ_{ni})
$$

is asymptotically $\rho$-equicontinuous. It converges in distribution in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$ provided the sequence of covariance functions converges pointwise on $\mathcal F\times\mathcal F$.

**Remark (measurability).** The maps

$$
(x_1,\ldots,x_{m_n})\longmapsto
\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}e_i\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^p\right|,
\qquad p=1,2,
$$

are measurable for every $\delta>0$, every $(e_1,\ldots,e_{m_n})\in\{-1,0,1\}^{m_n}$, and every $n$.
