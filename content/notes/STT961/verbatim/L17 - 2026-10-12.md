# 1. Shattering and the VC Index - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.6.1, pp. 134-135

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.6. Vapnik-Cervonenkis Classes of Sets and Functions (VC Classes)

**Definition.** Let $\mathcal C$ be a collection of subsets of a set $\mathcal X$.

An arbitrary set of $n$ points $\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$ has $2^n$ subsets. We say $\mathcal C$ picks out a subset of $\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$ if this set can be formed as

$$
C\cap\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}
\qquad\text{for some }C\in\mathcal C.
$$

$\mathcal C$ shatters $\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$ if each of its $2^n$ subsets can be picked out by $\mathcal C$.

The VC index of a class $\mathcal C$ is the smallest $n$ for which no set of size $n$ is shattered by $\mathcal C$.

$$
\Delta_n(\mathcal C,x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=\operatorname{card}\{C\cap\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}:C\in\mathcal C\},
$$

$$
V(\mathcal C)=\inf\left\{n:\max_{x_1,\ldots,x_n}
\Delta_n(\mathcal C,x_1,\ldots,x_n)<2^n\right\}.
$$

A collection $\mathcal C$ is called a VC class of sets if $V(\mathcal C)<\infty$.

# 2. Half-Lines and Intervals - van der Vaart & Wellner, Example 2.6.1, p. 135

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Example 1.**

$$
\mathcal C=\{(-\infty,c]:c\in\mathbb R\}.
$$

For $n=1$, both $\varnothing$ and $\{x_1\}$ can be picked out.

For $n=2$, take $x_1<x_2$. The subsets $\varnothing$, $\{x_1\}$, and $\{x_1,x_2\}$ can be picked out. It is impossible to pick out $\{x_2\}$ alone.

$$
V(\mathcal C)=2.
$$

**Example 2.**

$$
\mathcal C=\{[a,b]:a<b,\ a,b\in\mathbb R\}.
$$

For $n=1$ and $n=2$, the points can be shattered. For $n=3$, with $x_1<x_2<x_3$, an interval cannot pick out $x_1$ and $x_3$ alone.

$$
V(\mathcal C)=3.
$$

# 3. Half-Spaces Through the Origin - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.6.5, dimension argument related to Lemma 2.6.15, p. 146

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Example 3.** The space is $S=\mathbb R^d$, and

$$
\mathcal H=\{\text{all half-spaces with boundary passing through }0\}.
$$

**Claim.** $V(\mathcal H)=d+1$.

We need to show that there exist $d$ vectors in $\mathbb R^d$ that are shattered by $\mathcal H$, and that every set of $d+1$ vectors in $\mathbb R^d$ cannot be shattered by $\mathcal H$.

Let

$$
v_i=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0),\qquad i=1,\ldots,d,
$$

with $1$ in the $i$th coordinate. For every $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}$, take $\lambda_i>0$ for $i\in J$ and $\lambda_i<0$ for $i\notin J$. Then $\lambda\in\mathbb R^d$ and

$$
\langle\lambda,v_i\rangle=\lambda_i,
$$

$$
\lambda_i>0\iff i\in J\iff\langle\lambda,v_i\rangle>0
\iff v_i\in H,
$$

where

$$
H=\{h:\langle\lambda,h\rangle\geq0\}\in\mathcal H.
$$

Let $\{x_1,\ldots,x_{d+1}\}$ be arbitrary vectors in $\mathbb R^d$. After relabeling, there exist $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\in\mathbb R$ such that

$$
x_{d+1}=\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i x_i.
$$

Suppose we can shatter this set by $\mathcal H$. Consider

$$
J=\{i:1\leq i\leq d,\ \alpha_i\geq0\}\subset\{1,\ldots,d+1\}.
$$

There is a half-space $H\in\mathcal H$ with $x_i\in H$ if and only if $i\in J$. Thus $x_{d+1}\notin H$. Write

$$
H=\{h:\langle h,\ell\rangle\geq0\}.
$$

But

$$
\langle x_{d+1},\ell\rangle
=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i x_i,\ell\right\rangle
=\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i\langle x_i,\ell\rangle\geq0.
$$

For $i\in J$, both factors are nonnegative; for $i\notin J$, both are negative. Consequently $x_{d+1}\in H$, a contradiction.

# 5. Sign Sets of a Finite-Dimensional Function Space - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.6.5, Lemma 2.6.17(v), p. 147

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Example 6.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a $d$-dimensional linear space of functions on $S$. Define

$$
\mathcal C=\bigl\{\{x\in S:f(x)\geq0\}:f\in\mathcal F\bigr\}.
$$

**Claim.** $V(\mathcal C)=d+1$.

**Proof.** There is a linearly independent basis $g_1,\ldots,g_d$ in $\mathcal F$. For every $f\in\mathcal F$,

$$
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i g_i(x)
\qquad\text{for some }\alpha\in\mathbb R^d,\quad x\in S.
$$

Define

$$
G:S\to\mathbb R^d,\qquad G(x)=(g_1(x),\ldots,g_d(x)).
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{x:f(x)\geq0\}
&=\left\{x:\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i g_i(x)\geq0\right\}\\
&=\{x:\langle\alpha,G(x)\rangle\geq0\}
=G^{-1}(H_\alpha),
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
H_\alpha=\{h:\langle h,\alpha\rangle\geq0\}.
$$

Both inclusions hold, so

$$
\mathcal C=G^{-1}(\mathcal H),\qquad V(\mathcal C)=V(\mathcal H)=d+1.
$$
