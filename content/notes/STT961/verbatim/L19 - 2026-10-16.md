# 1. Smooth Function Classes - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.7.1, p. 154

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.7. Bracketing Numbers

For $\alpha>0$, denote by $\underline\alpha$ the greatest integer strictly smaller than $\alpha$.

$$
0<\alpha\leq1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\underline\alpha=0.
$$

For $k=(k_1,\ldots,k_d)$, with nonnegative integer coordinates, denote

$$
k_\bullet=\sum_{m=1}^d k_m.
$$

Define the operators

$$
D^k=\frac{\partial^{k_\bullet}}
{\partial x_1^{k_1}\partial x_2^{k_2}\cdots\partial x_d^{k_d}}.
$$

For a function $f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R$, $\mathcal X\subset\mathbb R^d$, define

$$
\|f\|_\alpha
:=\max_{k:k_\bullet\leq\underline\alpha}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X^\circ}|D^kf(x)|
+\max_{k:k_\bullet=\underline\alpha}
\sup_{\substack{x,y\in\mathcal X^\circ\\x\ne y}}
\frac{|D^kf(x)-D^kf(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\alpha-\underline\alpha}}.
$$

Here $\mathcal X^\circ$ is the interior of $\mathcal X$.

Let $C_M^\alpha(\mathcal X)$ be the set of continuous functions $f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R$ with

$$
\|f\|_\alpha\leq M.
$$

# 2. The Uniform Covering Bound - van der Vaart & Wellner, Theorem 2.7.1, p. 155

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.** Let $\mathcal X$ be a bounded, convex subset of $\mathbb R^d$ with nonempty interior. There is a constant $K$, depending on $\alpha$ and $d$ only, such that for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\log N\bigl(\varepsilon,C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X),\|\cdot\|_\infty\bigr)
\leq K\lambda(\mathcal X^1)
\left(\frac1\varepsilon\right)^{d/\alpha},
$$

where $\lambda(\mathcal X^1)$ is the Lebesgue measure of

$$
\mathcal X^1=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal X)<1\}.
$$

The diagram enlarges the convex set by one unit.

**Proof notation.**

$$
a\lesssim b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\leq Cb,
$$

where $C$ depends on $\alpha$ and $d$ only. Write $\beta=\underline\alpha$.

If $f\in C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X)$, then $f$ is continuous. Without loss of generality, take $\mathcal X$ open, so Taylor expansions work everywhere in $\mathcal X$.

# 3. Discretized Derivatives and Taylor Approximation - van der Vaart & Wellner, proof of Theorem 2.7.1, pp. 155-156

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Fix $\delta=\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}\leq1$. Form a $\delta$-net $x_1,\ldots,x_m\in\mathcal X$.

**Claim.**

$$
m\lesssim\lambda(\mathcal X^1)\delta^{-d}.
$$

This geometric covering argument is not proved here.

For every $k=(k_1,\ldots,k_d)$ with $k_\bullet\leq\beta$, and every $f\in C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X)$, introduce

$$
A_kf=\left(
\left\lfloor\frac{D^kf(x_1)}{\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}}\right\rfloor,
\ldots,
\left\lfloor\frac{D^kf(x_m)}{\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}}\right\rfloor
\right).
$$

The vector $\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}A_kf$ consists of the values $D^kf(x_i)$ discretized on a grid of mesh size $\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}$.

If $A_kf=A_kg$ for every $k$ with $k_\bullet\leq\beta$, choose $i$ such that $\|x-x_i\|\leq\delta$. Taylor expansion gives

$$
(f-g)(x)=\sum_{k_\bullet\leq\beta}
D^k(f-g)(x_i)\frac{(x-x_i)^k}{k!}+R,
$$

$$
|R|\lesssim\|x-x_i\|^\alpha\leq\delta^\alpha.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\begin{aligned}
|(f-g)(x)|
&\lesssim\sum_{k_\bullet\leq\beta}
\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}\frac{\delta^{k_\bullet}}{k!}
+\delta^\alpha\\
&\leq\delta^\alpha(1+e^d)\lesssim\delta^\alpha=\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus

$$
N\bigl(C\varepsilon,C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X),\|\cdot\|_\infty\bigr)
$$

is bounded by the number of different matrices $A_f$ whose rows are $A_kf$, $k_\bullet\leq\beta$.

# 4. Counting the Derivative Matrices - van der Vaart & Wellner, proof of Theorem 2.7.1, p. 156

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Since $f\in C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X)$,

$$
|D^kf(x_i)|\leq1\qquad\text{for every }i.
$$

There are at most $(1+\beta)^d$ multi-indices $k$ with $k_\bullet\leq\beta$. The number of possible values for one entry of $A_kf$ is at most

$$
\frac2{\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}}+2\leq2+2\delta^{-\alpha}.
$$

For each $x_i$, there are at most

$$
(2+2\delta^{-\alpha})^{(\beta+1)^d}
$$

possible columns of $A_f$. “We just discretize the derivatives.”

Without loss of generality, choose and order $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ so that, for every $j>1$, there is $i<j$ with

$$
\|x_i-x_j\|<2\delta.
$$

Then

$$
D^kf(x_j)=\sum_{k_\bullet+\ell_\bullet\leq\beta}
D^{k+\ell}f(x_i)\frac{(x_j-x_i)^\ell}{\ell!}+R,
$$

where

$$
|R|\lesssim\|x_j-x_i\|^{\alpha-k_\bullet}.
$$

Writing $A_{k+\ell}f(x_i)$ for the entry corresponding to $x_i$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|D^kf(x_j)-\sum_{k_\bullet+\ell_\bullet\leq\beta}
\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet-\ell_\bullet}
A_{k+\ell}f(x_i)\frac{(x_j-x_i)^\ell}{\ell!}\right|
&\lesssim\sum_{k_\bullet+\ell_\bullet\leq\beta}
\frac{\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet-\ell_\bullet}}{\ell!}(2\delta)^{\ell_\bullet}
+(2\delta)^{\alpha-k_\bullet}\\
&\lesssim\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the earlier column, $A_kf(x_j)$ ranges over integers in an interval of length proportional to

$$
\frac{\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}}{\delta^{\alpha-k_\bullet}}=1.
$$

Therefore the number of different $A_f$ is bounded by

$$
(2+2\delta^{-\alpha})^{(\beta+1)^d}C^{m-1}.
$$

Taking logarithms,

$$
\log N\bigl(C\varepsilon,C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X),\|\cdot\|_\infty\bigr)
\leq(m-1)\log C+(\beta+1)^d\log(2+2\delta^{-\alpha}).
$$

Here $m\lesssim\lambda(\mathcal X^1)\varepsilon^{-d/\alpha}$ and $\delta^{-\alpha}=1/\varepsilon$.

# 5. From Uniform Covers to Brackets - van der Vaart & Wellner, Corollary 2.7.2, p. 157

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Corollary.** Let $\mathcal X$ be a bounded, convex subset of $\mathbb R^d$ with nonempty interior. There is a constant $K$, depending on $d$, $\alpha$, and $\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal X)$, such that

$$
\log N_{[]}\bigl(\varepsilon,C_1^\alpha(\mathcal X),L_r(Q)\bigr)
\leq K\left(\frac1\varepsilon\right)^{d/\alpha}
$$

for every $r\geq1$, $\varepsilon>0$, and probability measure $Q$ on $\mathbb R^d$.
