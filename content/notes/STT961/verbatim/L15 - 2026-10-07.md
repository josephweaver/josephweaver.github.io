# 1. Completing Total Boundedness - §2.5.1, Theorem 2.5.2, pp. 127-129

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Recall the uniform entropy condition

$$
\int_0^\infty\sup_Q\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon\|F\|_{Q,2},\mathcal F,L_2(Q))}\,d\epsilon<\infty.
$$

We have proved

$$
\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal F_{\delta_n}}\xrightarrow{P^\star }0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0.
$$

It remains to show that $\mathcal F$ is totally bounded in $L_2(P)$. We showed

$$
\|(P_n-P)g^2\|_{\mathcal F_\infty}\xrightarrow{P^\star }0,
$$

where

$$
\mathcal F_\infty=\{f-g:f,g\in\mathcal F,\ \|f-g\|_{P,2}<\infty\}.
$$

Choose a sufficiently large $n$ and a realization with

$$
\|(P_n-P)g^2\|_{\mathcal F_\infty}<\epsilon^2.
$$

For $f,g\in\mathcal F$,

$$
P(f-g)^2=(P-P_n)(f-g)^2+P_n(f-g)^2.
$$

The first term is at most $\epsilon^2$. Thus an $\epsilon$-net with centers in $\mathcal F$ for $L_2(P_n)$ is a $\sqrt2\epsilon$-net for $L_2(P)$. The integral assumption gives a finite empirical cover, hence a finite population cover. Therefore $\mathcal F$ is totally bounded in $L_2(P)$.

# 2. The Classical Donsker Example - §2.5.1, Example 2.5.4, p. 129

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Example.**

$$
\mathcal F=\{1_{(-\infty,t]}:t\in\mathbb R\}.
$$

For every probability measure $Q$ and $0<\epsilon\le1$,

$$
N(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_2(Q))
\le N_{[]}(\epsilon^2,\mathcal F,L_1(Q))
\le\frac K{\epsilon^2}.
$$

For indicators $f,g$,

$$
\int(f-g)^2\,dQ<\epsilon^2
\iff\int|f-g|\,dQ<\epsilon^2.
$$

The envelope is $F=1$, and

$$
\int_0^1\sqrt{\log(K/\epsilon^2)}\,d\epsilon
=\int_0^1\sqrt{\log K-2\log\epsilon}\,d\epsilon<\infty.
$$

With $u=\log K-2\log\epsilon$,

$$
d\epsilon=-\frac{\sqrt K}{2}e^{-u/2}\,du,
$$

so

$$
\int_0^1\sqrt{\log(K/\epsilon^2)}\,d\epsilon
=\frac{\sqrt K}{2}\int_{\log K}^\infty u^{1/2}e^{-u/2}\,du
\le C\Gamma(3/2)<\infty.
$$

Also $P^\star 1=1<\infty$.

# 3. Weak L2 Size and the Bracketing Donsker Theorem - §2.5.2, Theorem 2.5.6, pp. 129-130

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Define

$$
\|f\|_{P,2,\infty}
:=\sup_{x>0}\bigl(x^2P(|f|>x)\bigr)^{1/2}.
$$

This is almost $L_2$, but not quite. It is not a norm: the triangle inequality fails. There is an equivalent norm, up to a constant.

**Donsker theorem with bracketing.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a class of measurable functions such that

$$
\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N_{[]}(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_{2,\infty}(P))}\,d\epsilon
+\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_2(P))}\,d\epsilon
<\infty.
$$

Moreover, assume the envelope $F$ possesses a weak second moment:

$$
P^\star (F>x)=o(x^{-2})\qquad(x\to\infty).
$$

Then $\mathcal F$ is $P$-Donsker.

# 4. Bernstein's Inequality and the Chaining Plan - §2.5.2, Equation (2.5.5), p. 130

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Proof idea.** Use Bernstein's inequality. For square-integrable bounded $f$, a valid version is

$$
P(|\mathbb G_nf|>x)
\le2\exp\left\{-\frac{x^2/2}{Pf^2+\frac{1}{3}\|f\|_\infty x/\sqrt n}\right\}.
$$

For a finite class $\mathcal H$ with $|\mathcal H|\ge2$, the Bernstein maximal inequality yields

$$
E\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal H}
\lesssim
\max_{f\in\mathcal H}\frac{\|f\|_\infty}{\sqrt n}\log|\mathcal H|
+\max_{f\in\mathcal H}\|f\|_{P,2}\sqrt{\log|\mathcal H|}.
$$

Balance the two terms at the scale

$$
\|f\|_\infty\asymp
\frac{\sqrt n\,\|f\|_{P,2}}{\sqrt{\log|\mathcal H|}}.
$$

To deal with an infinite class, use a chaining argument.

# 5. Nested Partitions and Finite Approximations - §2.5.2, Proof of Theorem 2.5.6, pp. 130-131

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

For every $q\in\mathbb N$, construct a partition

$$
\mathcal F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_q}\mathcal F_{qi}
$$

into disjoint sets such that

$$
\sum_{q=1}^\infty 2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q}<\infty,
$$

$$
\left\|\left(\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal F_{qi}}|f-g|\right)^\star\right\|_{P,2,\infty}
\le2^{-q},
\qquad
\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal F_{qi}}\|f-g\|_{P,2}\le2^{-q}.
$$

Why does this exist? Cover $\mathcal F$ with finitely many $L_2(P)$-balls and with $L_{2,\infty}(P)$-brackets at the corresponding dyadic scales. Intersect and make the resulting pieces disjoint. Do this in a nested way. The weighted entropy sum is controlled by the two finite entropy integrals.

Choose a fixed $f_{qi}\in\mathcal F_{qi}$, and set

$$
\pi_qf=f_{qi}\quad\text{if }f\in\mathcal F_{qi},
$$

$$
\Delta_qf=\left(\sup_{g,h\in\mathcal F_{qi}}|g-h|\right)^\star
\quad\text{if }f\in\mathcal F_{qi}.
$$

When $f$ runs through $\mathcal F$, both $\pi_qf$ and $\Delta_qf$ run through at most $N_q$ functions. The partition diagram groups each cell with its selected representative.

# 6. Tightness and the Stopped-Chain Decomposition - §1.5, Theorem 1.5.6, p. 36; §2.5.2, p. 131

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Recall: weak convergence in $\ell^\infty(T)$ is established through convergence of finite-dimensional marginals and asymptotic tightness.

**Theorem 1.5.6.** A net $X_\alpha:\Omega_\alpha\to\ell^\infty(T)$ is asymptotically tight if $X_\alpha(t)$ is asymptotically tight in $\mathbb R$ for every $t$, and for every $\epsilon,\eta>0$ there is a finite partition $T=\bigcup_{i=1}^kT_i$ such that

$$
\limsup_\alpha P^\star\left(
\sup_i\sup_{s,t\in T_i}|X_\alpha(s)-X_\alpha(t)|>\epsilon
\right)<\eta.
$$

For the present process it is enough to show

$$
\lim_{q_0\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
P^\star\left(\|\mathbb G_n(f-\pi_{q_0}f)\|_{\mathcal F}>\epsilon\right)=0
\qquad(\epsilon>0).
$$

Define

$$
a_q=\frac{2^{-q}}{\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}}.
$$

For $q>q_0$, define the pointwise indicator functions

$$
A_{q-1}f=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f\le\sqrt n a_{q_0},\ldots,
\Delta_{q-1}f\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}\},
$$

$$
B_qf=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f\le\sqrt n a_{q_0},\ldots,
\Delta_{q-1}f\le\sqrt n a_{q-1},\Delta_qf>\sqrt n a_q\},
$$

and

$$
B_{q_0}f=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f>\sqrt n a_{q_0}\}.
$$

Decompose pointwise:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f-\pi_{q_0}f
&=(f-\pi_{q_0}f)B_{q_0}f\\
&\quad+\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty(f-\pi_qf)B_qf\\
&\quad+\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty(\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f)A_{q-1}f.
\end{aligned}
$$

Either there is a unique $q_1$ for which $B_{q_1}f=1$, or every $B_qf$ is zero and the surviving chain continues through the $A_qf$ terms.
