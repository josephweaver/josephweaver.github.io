# 1. Maxima Under a Bernstein-Type Tail Bound - §2.2.2, Lemma 2.2.10, pp. 102-103

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Lemma.** Let $X_1,\ldots,X_m$ be arbitrary random variables with

$$
P(|X_i|>x)\le2\exp\left\{-\frac12\frac{x^2}{b+ax}\right\},
\qquad x>0,\ i=1,\ldots,m,
$$

for some $a,b>0$. Then

$$
\left\|\max_{1\le i\le m}X_i\right\|_{\psi_1}
\le K\left[a\log(1+m)+\sqrt b\sqrt{\log(1+m)}\right],
$$

where $K$ is a universal constant.

**Proof.** If $0<x\le b/a$, then $ax\le b$, and

$$
P(|X_i|>x)\le2\exp\left\{-\frac12\frac{x^2}{2b}\right\}
=2e^{-x^2/(4b)}.
$$

This is a Gaussian tail. If $x>b/a$, then $ax>b$, and

$$
P(|X_i|>x)\le2\exp\left\{-\frac12\frac{x^2}{2ax}\right\}
=2e^{-x/(4a)}.
$$

This is an exponential tail. Use $\psi_2$ for the Gaussian part and $\psi_1$ for the exponential part. Write

$$
U_i=X_i1\{|X_i|\le b/a\},\qquad
V_i=X_i1\{|X_i|>b/a\},\qquad X_i=U_i+V_i.
$$

By the triangle inequality, comparison of Orlicz norms, and the maximal inequality,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\max_iX_i\right\|_{\psi_1}
&\le\left\|\max_i|U_i|\right\|_{\psi_1}
+\left\|\max_i|V_i|\right\|_{\psi_1}\\
&\le C\left\|\max_i|U_i|\right\|_{\psi_2}
+\left\|\max_i|V_i|\right\|_{\psi_1}\\
&\le K\psi_2^{-1}(m)\max_i\|U_i\|_{\psi_2}
+K\psi_1^{-1}(m)\max_i\|V_i\|_{\psi_1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Recall

$$
\psi_1^{-1}(m)=\log(1+m),\qquad
\psi_2^{-1}(m)=\sqrt{\log(1+m)},
$$

and the earlier lemma

$$
P(|X|>x)\le K_0e^{-cx^p}
\implies
\|X\|_{\psi_p}\le\left(\frac{1+K_0}{c}\right)^{1/p}.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\max_iX_i\right\|_{\psi_1}
&\le K\sqrt{\log(1+m)}\sqrt{\frac{1+2}{1/(4b)}}
+K\log(1+m)\frac{1+2}{1/(4a)}\\
&\le\widetilde K\left[a\log(1+m)+\sqrt b\sqrt{\log(1+m)}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

# 2. Symmetrization and the Product Probability Space - §2.3.1, pp. 107-108

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Let $\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_n$ be i.i.d. Rademacher random variables, independent of $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$:

$$
P(\epsilon_i=1)=P(\epsilon_i=-1)=\frac12.
$$

Compare

$$
f\longmapsto(P_n-P)f=\int f\,dP_n-\int f\,dP,
\qquad P_nf=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf(X_i),
$$

with

$$
f\longmapsto P_n^\circ f:=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_if(X_i).
$$

**Idea:** limit theorems for $P_n-P$ correspond to limit theorems for $P_n^\circ$.

Conditionally on $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$, the process $f\mapsto P_n^\circ f$ is a Rademacher process, hence sub-Gaussian.

The original space is $(\mathcal X^n,\mathcal A^n,P^n)$. We now work on

$$
(\mathcal X^n,\mathcal A^n,P^n)\times(\mathcal Z,\mathcal G,Q).
$$

# 3. The First Symmetrization Inequality - §2.3.1, Lemma 2.3.1, pp. 108-109

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Lemma 1.** For every nondecreasing convex $\Phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ and class $\mathcal F$ of measurable functions,

$$
E^\star\Phi(\|P_n-P\|_{\mathcal F})
\le E^\star\Phi(2\|P_n^\circ\|_{\mathcal F}).
$$

**Proof.** Let $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)$ be an independent copy of $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$. Enlarge the space to

$$
(\mathcal X^n,\mathcal A^n,P^n)
\times(\mathcal Z,\mathcal G,Q)
\times(\mathcal X^n,\mathcal A^n,P^n).
$$

Since $Ef(X_i)=Ef(Y_i)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_n-P\|_{\mathcal F}
&=\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-Ef(Y_i))\right|\\
&=\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\left|E_Y\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-f(Y_i))\right|\\
&\le\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}E_Y\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-f(Y_i))\right|\\
&\le E_Y^\star\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-f(Y_i))\right|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Use monotonicity of $\Phi$, then Jensen's inequality, then take the outer expectation over $X$:

$$
E_X^\star\Phi(\|P_n-P\|_{\mathcal F})
\le E_X^\star E_Y^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-f(Y_i))\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)
\le E_{X,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(f(X_i)-f(Y_i))\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right).
$$

The differences are symmetric, so insert the independent signs:

$$
E^\star\Phi(\|P_n-P\|_{\mathcal F})
\le E_\epsilon E_{X,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_i(f(X_i)-f(Y_i))\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right).
$$

Put

$$
A=\left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_if(X_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F},
\qquad B=\left\|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_if(Y_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F}.
$$

By the triangle inequality, monotonicity, and convexity,

$$
\Phi(A+B)=\Phi\left(\frac12(2A)+\frac12(2B)\right)
\le\frac12\Phi(2A)+\frac12\Phi(2B).
$$

The $X$ and $Y$ terms have the same distribution. Thus the bound is

$$
E^\star\Phi\left(2\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_if(X_i)\right|\right)
=E^\star\Phi(2\|P_n^\circ\|_{\mathcal F}).
$$

# 4. Two-Sided Symmetrization for Centered Processes - §2.3.2, Lemma 2.3.6, pp. 111-112

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Lemma 2.** Let $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$ be independent stochastic processes with mean zero. Then

$$
E^\star\Phi\left(\frac12\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_iZ_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)
\le E^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)
\le E^\star\Phi\left(2\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\epsilon_i(Z_i-\mu_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)
$$

for every nondecreasing convex $\Phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ and arbitrary functions $\mu_i:\mathcal F\to\mathbb R$.

The second inequality follows by redoing the proof of Lemma 1.

For the first, let $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)$ be an independent copy of $(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)$. Fix any $(e_1,\ldots,e_n)\in\{-1,1\}^n$. Since $EY_i(f)=0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_Z^\star\Phi\left(\frac12\left\|\sum e_iZ_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)
&=E_Z^\star\Phi\left(\frac12\left\|\sum e_i(Z_i-E_YY_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)\\
&\le E_{Z,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\frac12\left\|\sum e_i(Z_i-Y_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)\\
&=E_{Z,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\frac12\left\|\sum(Z_i-Y_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)\\
&\le E_{Z,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\frac12\left\|\sum Z_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}
+\frac12\left\|\sum Y_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)\\
&\le\frac12E_{Z,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\sum Z_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)
+\frac12E_{Z,Y}^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\sum Y_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right)\\
&=E_Z^\star\Phi\left(\left\|\sum Z_i\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Average over the fixed sign vectors to obtain the left inequality.
