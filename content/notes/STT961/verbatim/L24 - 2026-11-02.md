# 1. The Random Entropy Central Limit Theorem - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, Theorem 2.11.1

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.**

**(1)**

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E^\star\left[
\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}^{2}I(\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}>\eta)
\right]\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\eta>0.
$$

**(2)**

$$
\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}
E\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^2\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0.
$$

**(3)**

$$
\int_0^{\delta_n}\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,d_n)}\,d\varepsilon
\overset{P^\star }{\longrightarrow}0
\qquad\text{for every }\delta_n\downarrow0,
$$

where

$$
d_n^2(f,g)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^2.
$$

Then

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}(Z_{ni}-EZ_{ni})
$$

is asymptotically $\rho$-equicontinuous and converges in distribution in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$ provided the covariance functions converge pointwise on $\mathcal F\times\mathcal F$.

# 2. Symmetrization and the Entropy Bound - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, Proof of Theorem 2.11.1

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Proof sketch.** The Lindeberg condition for $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal F}$ implies the Lindeberg condition for the marginals. Convergence of covariance functions then gives weak convergence of the finite-dimensional marginals to those of a Gaussian process.

Consider the proof of asymptotic equicontinuity. Set

$$
Z_{ni}^{\circ}=Z_{ni}-EZ_{ni}
$$

and take an arbitrary $\delta_n\downarrow0$.

For fixed $t>0$, sufficiently large $n$, and $f,g$ with $\rho(f,g)<\delta_n$, Chebyshev's inequality gives

$$
P\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}
\bigl(Z_{ni}^{\circ}(f)-Z_{ni}^{\circ}(g)\bigr)\right|>\frac t2\right)
\leq
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}E\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)^2}{(t/2)^2}
\leq\frac12.
$$

The numerator converges to zero uniformly over these pairs.

By the probability symmetrization lemma,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&P^\star\left(\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\bigl(Z_{ni}^{\circ}(f)-Z_{ni}^{\circ}(g)\bigr)\right|>t\right)\\
&\qquad\leq4P\left(\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)\right|>\frac t4\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_{m_n}$ are independent Rademacher variables, independent of the processes.

Here the probability lower bound entering symmetrization is

$$
\beta_n=
\inf_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}
P\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}
\bigl(Z_{ni}^{\circ}(f)-Z_{ni}^{\circ}(g)\bigr)\right|\leq\frac t2\right)
\geq\frac12.
$$

For fixed values of the processes, define $A_n\subset\mathbb R^{m_n}$ by

$$
A_n=\left\{
\bigl(Z_{n1}(f)-Z_{n1}(g),\ldots,Z_{nm_n}(f)-Z_{nm_n}(g)\bigr):
(f,g)\in\mathcal F\times\mathcal F,\ \rho(f,g)<\delta_n
\right\}.
$$

We have the Rademacher process

$$
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i\epsilon_i:a\in A_n\right\}.
$$

By Hoeffding's inequality, this process is sub-Gaussian for the Euclidean metric on $A_n$. By the Orlicz norm and entropy theorem from §2.2,

$$
E_\epsilon\sup_{\rho(f,g)<\delta_n}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i\bigl(Z_{ni}(f)-Z_{ni}(g)\bigr)\right|
\lesssim
\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,A_n,\|\cdot\|)}\,d\varepsilon.
$$

Moreover,

$$
N(\varepsilon,A_n,\|\cdot\|)
\leq N^2(\varepsilon/2,\mathcal F,d_n).
$$

Set

$$
\theta_n^2=
\sup_{a\in A_n}\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2
=
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}.
$$

For $\varepsilon>\theta_n$, $A_n$ is contained in an $\varepsilon$-ball, so the corresponding entropy integrand vanishes. Thus it is enough to control

$$
\int_0^{\theta_n}
\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon/2,\mathcal F,d_n)}\,d\varepsilon.
$$

If $\theta_n\overset{P^\star }{\longrightarrow}0$, the random entropy assumption makes this integral converge to zero in outer probability. Markov's inequality applied conditionally to the Rademacher process then gives the required asymptotic equicontinuity.

# 3. Controlling the Random Radius - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.11.1, Proof of Theorem 2.11.1 and Problem 2.11.1

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

We prove $\theta_n\overset{P^\star }{\longrightarrow}0$.

By the Lindeberg condition, there exists a sequence $\eta_n\downarrow0$ such that

$$
E^\star\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2
I\bigl(\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}^{*}>\eta_n\bigr)\right\|_{A_n}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

By truncation, we may assume

$$
\|Z_{ni}\|_{\mathcal F}\leq\eta_n
\qquad\text{for every }i.
$$

Fix $Z_{n1},\ldots,Z_{nm_n}$ and consider an $\varepsilon$-net $B_n$ of $A_n$ for the Euclidean metric. For every $a\in A_n$, there exists $b\in B_n$ with

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i a_i^2\right|
&=
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i(a_i-b_i)^2
+2\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i(a_i-b_i)b_i
+\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i b_i^2\right|\\
&\leq\varepsilon^2+2\varepsilon\|b\|
+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i b_i^2\right|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hoeffding's inequality and the maximal inequality for $\psi_2$ first give

$$
E_\epsilon\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}
\lesssim\varepsilon^2+2\varepsilon\sup_{b\in B_n}\|b\|
+\sqrt{1+\log|B_n|}\,\eta_n\sup_{b\in B_n}\|b\|.
$$

Since $B_n\subset A_n$,

$$
\sup_{b\in B_n}\|b\|
\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}^{1/2},
$$

and hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_\epsilon\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}
\lesssim{}&\varepsilon^2
+2\varepsilon\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}^{1/2}\\
&+\sqrt{1+\log|B_n|}\,\eta_n
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The size of the net can be chosen to satisfy

$$
|B_n|\leq N^2(\varepsilon/2,\mathcal F,d_n).
$$

For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, this is bounded in probability by the random entropy condition, as in Problem 2.11.1.

Consequently, for some constant $K$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P\left(\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}\epsilon_i a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}>t\right)
\leq{}&P^\star (|B_n|>M)\\
&+\frac Kt\left[
\varepsilon^2+
\bigl(\varepsilon+\eta_n\sqrt{\log M}\bigr)
E\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}a_i^2\right\|_{A_n}^{1/2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The next step uses the Hoffmann–Jørgensen inequality for moments.

# 4. Hoffmann Jørgensen Inequalities for Moments - van der Vaart & Wellner Appendix A.1, Proposition A.1.5

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Let $0<p<\infty$, and suppose $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ are independent stochastic processes indexed by an arbitrary set $T$. Let

$$
S_k=\sum_{i=1}^kX_i.
$$

Then there exist a constant $C_p$ and $0<u_p<1$ such that

$$
E^\star\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\|S_k\|^p
\leq C_p\left(
E^\star\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\|X_k\|^p
+\bigl[F_n^{-1}(u_p)\bigr]^p
\right),
$$

where $F_n^{-1}$ is the quantile function of the random variable

$$
\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\|S_k\|^\star .
$$

In particular, if $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ are symmetric, there exist a constant $K_p$ and $0<v_p<1$ such that

$$
E^\star\|S_n\|^p
\leq K_p\left(
E^\star\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\|X_k\|^p
+\bigl[G_n^{-1}(v_p)\bigr]^p
\right),
$$

where $G_n^{-1}$ is the quantile function of $\|S_n\|^\star $.

For $p\geq1$, the last inequality also holds for mean-zero stochastic processes.

Take $p=1$.
