# 1. Portmanteau Theorem — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.3, Theorem 1.3.4

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Portmanteau Theorem

The following statements are equivalent:

**(i)**

$$
X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow L
$$

**(ii)**

$$
\liminf_\alpha P_\star (X_\alpha\in G)\geq L(G)
\qquad\text{for every open }G.
$$

**(iii)**

$$
\limsup_\alpha P^\star (X_\alpha\in F)\leq L(F)
\qquad\forall\text{ closed }F.
$$

**(iv)**

$$
\liminf_\alpha E_\star f(X_\alpha)\geq\int f\,dL
$$

for every lower semicontinuous $f$ bounded below.

**(v)**

$$
\limsup_\alpha E^\star f(X_\alpha)\leq\int f\,dL
$$

for every upper semicontinuous $f$ bounded above.

**(vi)**

$$
\lim_\alpha P^\star (X_\alpha\in B)
=
\lim_\alpha P_\star (X_\alpha\in B)
=
L(B)
$$

for every Borel set $B$ with

$$
L(\partial B)
=
L(\overline B-B^\circ)=0.
$$

**(vii)**

$$
\liminf_\alpha E_\star f(X_\alpha)\geq\int f\,dL
$$

for every bounded Lipschitz continuous $f\geq0$.

# 3. Continuous Mapping Theorem — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.3, Theorem 1.3.6

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Cor. to Portmanteau Th.**

For every function $g$ continuous a.s. with $X$,

$$
X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow X
\implies
g(X_\alpha)\rightsquigarrow g(X).
$$

**Continuous Mapping Th.**

Let $g:\mathbb D\to\mathbb E$ be continuous at $\forall$ point $x\in\mathbb D_0\subset\mathbb D$.

If $X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow X$ and $X$ takes values in $\mathbb D_0$, then

$$
g(X_\alpha)\rightsquigarrow g(X).
$$

# 4. Asymptotic Measurability and Asymptotic Tightness — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.3, Definition 1.3.7 and Lemma 1.3.8

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Def.** The net of maps $X_\alpha$ is asymp. measurable $\iff$

$$
E^\star f(X_\alpha)-E_\star f(X_\alpha)\to0
$$

for $\forall f\in C_b(\mathbb D)$.

**Def.** The net $X_\alpha$ is asymp. tight if for $\forall\varepsilon>0$

$\exists$ compact $K$:

$$
\liminf_\alpha P_\star (X_\alpha\in K^\delta)
\geq1-\varepsilon
\qquad\text{for }\forall\delta>0,
$$

where

$$
K^\delta=\text{$\delta$-enlargement of }K
=\{y\in\mathbb D:d(y,K)<\delta\}.
$$

---

**Lemma.**

1. If $X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow X$, then $X_\alpha$ is asymp. measurable.

2. If $X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow X$, then

   $$
   X_\alpha\text{ is asymp. tight}
   \iff X\text{ is tight}.
   $$

**Remark:**

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0,\quad
\exists\text{ compact }K:
\quad P(X\in K)\geq1-\varepsilon.
$$

# 5. Prohorov's Theorem — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.3, Theorem 1.3.9

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Prohorov's Theorem

**(i)** If the net $X_\alpha$ is asymp. tight and asymp. measurable, then it has a subnet $X_{\alpha(\beta)}$ that converges in law to a tight Borel law.

**(ii)** If the sequence $X_n$ is asymp. tight and asymp. measurable, then it has a subsequence $X_{n_k}$ that converges in law to a tight Borel law.

# 6. Other Properties of Weak Convergence — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.3, Theorem 1.3.10 and Lemma 1.3.12

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Other Properties of $\rightsquigarrow$

**1.** Let $\mathbb D_0\subset\mathbb D$ be an arbitrary set. Both $\mathbb D_0,\mathbb D$ are equipped with the same metric. Let $X_\alpha,X$ take values in $\mathbb D_0$.

Then

$$
X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow X
\text{ as maps in }\mathbb D_0
\iff
X_\alpha\rightsquigarrow X
\text{ as maps in }\mathbb D.
$$

**2.** Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be finite Borel measures on $\mathbb D$.

If

$$
\int f\,dL_1=\int f\,dL_2
\qquad\forall f\in C_b(\mathbb D),
$$

then

$$
L_1=L_2.
$$

**3.** Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be tight Borel prob. measures on $\mathbb D$.

If

$$
\int f\,dL_1=\int f\,dL_2
$$

for $\forall f$ in a vector lattice $\mathcal F\subset C_b(\mathbb D)$ that contains const. functions and separates points in $\mathbb D$,

then

$$
L_1=L_2.
$$

**Def.** Vector lattice $\mathcal F$ is a vector space closed under taking positive parts:

$$
f\in\mathcal F,\qquad f^+=f\vee0\in\mathcal F.
$$

**Def.** $\mathcal F$ separates points in $\mathbb D$ if

$$
\forall x\ne y\in\mathbb D,\quad
\exists f\in\mathcal F:
f(x)\ne f(y).
$$

# 7. Asymptotic Measurability from a Separating Algebra — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.3, Lemma 1.3.13

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**4.** Let the net $X_\alpha$ be asymp. tight and

$$
E^\star f(X_\alpha)-E_\star f(X_\alpha)\to0
$$

$\forall f$ in subalgebra $\mathcal F\subset C_b(\mathbb D)$ that separates points in $\mathbb D$.

Then $X_\alpha$ is asymp. measurable.

**Def.** $\mathcal F$ is algebra if $\mathcal F$ is a vector space that is closed under product:

$$
f,g\in\mathcal F\implies fg\in\mathcal F.

# 8. Spaces of Bounded Functions — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.5

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Ch. 1.5 Spaces of Bounded Functions

Let $T$ be an arbitrary set.

The space $\ell^\infty(T)$ is the set of all uniformly bounded real functions on $T$.

$$
z:T\to\mathbb R:
\quad
\|z\|_T=\sup_{t\in T}|z(t)|<\infty.
$$

$\ell^\infty(T)$ is a metric space wrt the uniform distance (metric)

$$
d(z_1,z_2)=\|z_1-z_2\|_T.
$$

# 9. Stochastic Processes and Sample Paths — van der Vaart & Wellner §1.5

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Def.** A stochastic process is an indexed collection

$$
\{X(t):t\in T\}
$$

of random variables defined on the same probability space

$$
(\Omega,\mathcal A,P).
$$

Every

$$
X(t):\Omega\to\mathbb R
$$

is measurable.

If every sample path

$$
t\mapsto X(t,\omega)
$$

is bounded, then the stochastic process yields a map

$$
X:\Omega\to\ell^\infty(T).
$$
