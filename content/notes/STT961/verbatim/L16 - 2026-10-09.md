# 1. Recalling the Bracketing Theorem and Decomposition - §2.5.2, Theorem 2.5.6, pp. 130-131

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Donsker theorem with bracketing, part 2.** Recall the conditions

$$
\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N_{[]}(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_{2,\infty}(P))}\,d\epsilon
+\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_2(P))}\,d\epsilon<\infty,
$$

$$
P^\star (F>x)=o(x^{-2})\qquad(x\to\infty),
$$

which imply that $\mathcal F$ is $P$-Donsker.

Use the nested partitions $\mathcal F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_q}\mathcal F_{qi}$ and the functions $\pi_qf,\Delta_qf$ from the preceding lecture. Recall

$$
a_q=\frac{2^{-q}}{\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}},
\qquad B_{q_0}f=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f>\sqrt n a_{q_0}\},
$$

$$
A_{q-1}f=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f\le\sqrt n a_{q_0},\ldots,
\Delta_{q-1}f\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}\},
$$

$$
B_qf=A_{q-1}f\,1\{\Delta_qf>\sqrt n a_q\}.
$$

The decomposition is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f-\pi_{q_0}f
&=\underbrace{(f-\pi_{q_0}f)B_{q_0}f}_{1}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty(f-\pi_qf)B_qf}_{2}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty(\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f)A_{q-1}f}_{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The goal is

$$
\lim_{q_0\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
P^\star (\|\mathbb G_n(f-\pi_{q_0}f)\|_{\mathcal F}>\epsilon)=0
\qquad(\epsilon>0).
$$

# 3. The Stopping Remainders - §2.5.2, Theorem 2.5.6, p. 132; Lemmas 2.2.9-2.2.10, pp. 102-103

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Part 2.** Since the partitions are nested,

$$
\Delta_qf B_qf\le\Delta_{q-1}f B_qf.
$$

By the weak-$L_2$ tail bound,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n a_q\,P(\Delta_qf B_qf)
&\le2\sup_{x>0}x^2P(\Delta_qf>x)\\
&=2\|\Delta_qf\|_{P,2,\infty}^2
\le2\cdot2^{-2q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

On $B_qf=1$, the preceding test has passed, so for $q>q_0$,

$$
\Delta_{q-1}f B_qf\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
P[(\Delta_qf B_qf)^2]
&\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}
P\left[\Delta_qf1\{\Delta_qf>\sqrt n a_q\}\right]\\
&\le2\frac{a_{q-1}}{a_q}2^{-2q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Fact used to bound the residual.** If $|r|\le h$ with $h\ge0$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathbb G_nr|
&\le\sqrt n P_nh+\sqrt n Ph\\
&=\mathbb G_nh+2\sqrt n Ph\\
&\le|\mathbb G_nh|+2\sqrt n Ph.
\end{aligned}
$$

Apply this to $r=(f-\pi_qf)B_qf$ and $h=\Delta_qf B_qf$. Taking suprema and summing,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E^\star\left\|\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\mathbb G_n\bigl((f-\pi_qf)B_qf\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}
&\le\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty E^\star\|\mathbb G_n(\Delta_qf B_qf)\|_{\mathcal F}\\
&\quad+2\sqrt n\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty\|P(\Delta_qf B_qf)\|_{\mathcal F}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Recall Bernstein's inequality for independent centered $Y_i$, $|Y_i|\le M$, and $V\ge\operatorname{Var}(\sum_iY_i)$:

$$
P\left(\left|\sum_iY_i\right|>x\right)
\le2\exp\left\{-\frac{x^2/2}{V+Mx/3}\right\}.
$$

For $m$ random variables $W_1,\ldots,W_m$ each satisfying this Bernstein tail bound with the same $M,V$, the recalled Orlicz maximal inequality is

$$
\left\|\max_{1\le j\le m}|W_j|\right\|_{\psi_1}
\le K\left[\frac M3\log(1+m)+\sqrt V\sqrt{\log(1+m)}\right].
$$

For a finite class $\mathcal H$, the associated Orlicz maximal bound gives

$$
E\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal H}
\lesssim\max_{h\in\mathcal H}\frac{\|h\|_\infty}{\sqrt n}\log|\mathcal H|
+\max_{h\in\mathcal H}\|h\|_{P,2}\sqrt{\log|\mathcal H|}.
$$

Here the relevant finite class is

$$
\mathcal H_q=\{\Delta_qf B_qf:f\in\mathcal F\},
\qquad |\mathcal H_q|\le N_q.
$$

For an empirical-process summand,

$$
Y_i=\frac{h(X_i)-Ph}{\sqrt n},\qquad
|Y_i|\le\frac{2\|h\|_\infty}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb G_nh)=\operatorname{Var}(h(X_1))\le Ph^2.
$$

Combining the preceding estimates, up to a universal constant,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E^\star\left\|\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\mathbb G_n\bigl((f-\pi_qf)B_qf\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\left[
 a_{q-1}\log N_q
 +\sqrt{\frac{a_{q-1}}{a_q}}\,2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q}
 +\frac4{a_q}2^{-2q}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute $a_q=2^{-q}/\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}$. These terms are bounded by a constant times the tail of the convergent entropy series. More explicitly,

$$
a_{q-1}\log N_q=2\cdot2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q},
$$

$$
2^{-q}\sqrt{\frac{a_{q-1}}{a_q}}\sqrt{\log N_q}
=\sqrt2\,2^{-q}(\log N_q\log N_{q+1})^{1/4}
\le\sqrt2\,2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}},
$$

$$
\frac4{a_q}2^{-2q}=4\cdot2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}.
$$

Thus the total is at most

$$
K\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}
\longrightarrow0\qquad(q_0\to\infty),
$$

independently of $n$.

# 4. The Surviving Chain and Completion - §2.5.2, Proof of Theorem 2.5.6, p. 132

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Part 3.** Nestedness gives

$$
|\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f|A_{q-1}f
\le\Delta_{q-1}f A_{q-1}f
\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}.
$$

Also, by the choice of the partitions,

$$
\|\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f\|_{P,2}\le2^{-q+1}.
$$

Use Bernstein's inequality and the finite-class maximal bound again:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E^\star\left\|\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\mathbb G_n\bigl((\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f)A_{q-1}f\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\left[
\frac{\sqrt n a_{q-1}}{\sqrt n}\log N_q
+2^{-q+1}\sqrt{\log N_q}
\right]\\
&\quad\le K\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q}
\longrightarrow0\qquad(q_0\to\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

Together the three parts establish the required approximation and asymptotic tightness, completing the Donsker theorem.
