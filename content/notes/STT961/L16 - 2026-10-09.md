# 1. Recalling the Bracketing Theorem and Decomposition - §2.5.2, Theorem 2.5.6, pp. 130-131

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Donsker theorem with bracketing, part 2.** Recall the conditions

$$
\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N_{[]}(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_{2,\infty}(P))}\,d\epsilon
+\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal F,L_2(P))}\,d\epsilon<\infty,
$$

$$
P^*(F>x)=o(x^{-2})\qquad(x\to\infty),
$$

which imply that $\mathcal F$ is $P$-Donsker.

Use the nested partitions $\mathcal F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_q}\mathcal F_{qi}$ and the functions $\pi_qf,\Delta_qf$ from the preceding lecture. Recall

$$
a_q=\frac{2^{-q}}{\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}},
\qquad B_{q_0}f=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f>\sqrt n a_{q_0}\},
$$

$$
A_{q-1}f=1\{\Delta_{q_0}f\le\sqrt n a_{q_0},\ldots,
\Delta_{q-1}f\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}\},
$$

$$
B_qf=A_{q-1}f\,1\{\Delta_qf>\sqrt n a_q\}.
$$

The decomposition is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f-\pi_{q_0}f
&=\underbrace{(f-\pi_{q_0}f)B_{q_0}f}_{1}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty(f-\pi_qf)B_qf}_{2}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty(\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f)A_{q-1}f}_{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

*Correction: The recalled second term in my notes writes $f-\pi_{q_0}f$ inside every summand, and the indicator list has mismatched indices. The residual at stopping level $q$ must be $f-\pi_qf$, and $A_{q-1}$ tests levels only through $q-1$. Corrected against the decomposition and definitions on p. 131, also written correctly in the preceding lecture.*

The goal is

$$
\lim_{q_0\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
P^*(\|\mathbb G_n(f-\pi_{q_0}f)\|_{\mathcal F}>\epsilon)=0
\qquad(\epsilon>0).
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The preceding lecture constructed the nested partitions and split the approximation error into three pieces. This lecture applies the empirical process to each piece separately. The first is easy, the second contains the main balancing calculation, and the third is similar to the second. The professor again stressed that the thresholds were not guessed in their final form: they are chosen so that the estimates obtained later have compatible sizes.

$$
|f-\pi_{q_0}f|B_{q_0}f

$$

Therefore

$$
E^*\left\|\mathbb G_n\bigl((f-\pi_{q_0}f)B_{q_0}f\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}

# 3. The Stopping Remainders - §2.5.2, Theorem 2.5.6, p. 132; Lemmas 2.2.9-2.2.10, pp. 102-103

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Part 2.** Since the partitions are nested,

$$
\Delta_qf B_qf\le\Delta_{q-1}f B_qf.
$$

By the weak-$L_2$ tail bound,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt n a_q\,P(\Delta_qf B_qf)
&\le2\sup_{x>0}x^2P(\Delta_qf>x)\\
&=2\|\Delta_qf\|_{P,2,\infty}^2
\le2\cdot2^{-2q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

On $B_qf=1$, the preceding test has passed, so for $q>q_0$,

$$
\Delta_{q-1}f B_qf\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
P[(\Delta_qf B_qf)^2]
&\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}
P\left[\Delta_qf1\{\Delta_qf>\sqrt n a_q\}\right]\\
&\le2\frac{a_{q-1}}{a_q}2^{-2q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Fact used to bound the residual.** If $|r|\le h$ with $h\ge0$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathbb G_nr|
&\le\sqrt n P_nh+\sqrt n Ph\\
&=\mathbb G_nh+2\sqrt n Ph\\
&\le|\mathbb G_nh|+2\sqrt n Ph.
\end{aligned}
$$

Apply this to $r=(f-\pi_qf)B_qf$ and $h=\Delta_qf B_qf$. Taking suprema and summing,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E^*\left\|\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\mathbb G_n\bigl((f-\pi_qf)B_qf\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}
&\le\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty E^*\|\mathbb G_n(\Delta_qf B_qf)\|_{\mathcal F}\\
&\quad+2\sqrt n\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty\|P(\Delta_qf B_qf)\|_{\mathcal F}.
\end{aligned}
$$

*Textbook clarification: The handwritten general supremum manipulation is applied cell by cell using the majorant $\Delta_qf B_qf$, then maximized. It does not replace the whole class by one arbitrary global supremum before counting its functions. This is the first bound in the second-term argument on p. 132.*

Recall Bernstein's inequality for independent centered $Y_i$, $|Y_i|\le M$, and $V\ge\operatorname{Var}(\sum_iY_i)$:

$$
P\left(\left|\sum_iY_i\right|>x\right)
\le2\exp\left\{-\frac{x^2/2}{V+Mx/3}\right\}.
$$

For $m$ random variables $W_1,\ldots,W_m$ each satisfying this Bernstein tail bound with the same $M,V$, the recalled Orlicz maximal inequality is

$$
\left\|\max_{1\le j\le m}|W_j|\right\|_{\psi_1}
\le K\left[\frac M3\log(1+m)+\sqrt V\sqrt{\log(1+m)}\right].
$$

For a finite class $\mathcal H$, the associated Orlicz maximal bound gives

$$
E\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal H}
\lesssim\max_{h\in\mathcal H}\frac{\|h\|_\infty}{\sqrt n}\log|\mathcal H|
+\max_{h\in\mathcal H}\|h\|_{P,2}\sqrt{\log|\mathcal H|}.
$$

Here the relevant finite class is

$$
\mathcal H_q=\{\Delta_qf B_qf:f\in\mathcal F\},
\qquad |\mathcal H_q|\le N_q.
$$

For an empirical-process summand,

$$
Y_i=\frac{h(X_i)-Ph}{\sqrt n},\qquad
|Y_i|\le\frac{2\|h\|_\infty}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb G_nh)=\operatorname{Var}(h(X_1))\le Ph^2.
$$

*Correction: My notes conflate the bound on the complete empirical-process sum with the bound $M$ needed for its individual centered summands. The summand bound is $2\|h\|_\infty/\sqrt n$, whereas the variance bound is for the sum. This is the application of Lemma 2.2.9, p. 102, underlying (2.5.5), p. 130.*

Combining the preceding estimates, up to a universal constant,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E^*\left\|\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\mathbb G_n\bigl((f-\pi_qf)B_qf\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\left[
 a_{q-1}\log N_q
 +\sqrt{\frac{a_{q-1}}{a_q}}\,2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q}
 +\frac4{a_q}2^{-2q}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute $a_q=2^{-q}/\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}$. These terms are bounded by a constant times the tail of the convergent entropy series. More explicitly,

$$
a_{q-1}\log N_q=2\cdot2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q},
$$

$$
2^{-q}\sqrt{\frac{a_{q-1}}{a_q}}\sqrt{\log N_q}
=\sqrt2\,2^{-q}(\log N_q\log N_{q+1})^{1/4}
\le\sqrt2\,2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}},
$$

$$
\frac4{a_q}2^{-2q}=4\cdot2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}.
$$

Thus the total is at most

$$
K\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_{q+1}}
\longrightarrow0\qquad(q_0\to\infty),
$$

independently of $n$.

*Correction: The handwritten simplification suppresses the adjacent indices in $\log N_q$ and $\log N_{q+1}$. Their ratio is not assumed bounded. Keeping the shifted entropy tail, and then reindexing the convergent series, justifies the limit without that extra assumption. This supplies the algebra for the entropy-tail bound on p. 132.*

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

Nestedness ensures that the supremum over a smaller cell cannot exceed the supremum over its parent. The indicator for a first failure also tells us that the preceding level was still bounded. These two observations supply the first- and second-moment bounds needed by Bernstein.

The functions to which the finite-class maximal inequality is applied are the diameter functions multiplied by the stopping indicators, not the original members of $\mathcal F$. There are finitely many at each level because they are constant as choices of functions on partition cells.

This is where the threshold design becomes visible. The boundedness term, variance term, and additional mean term all reduce to entropy-series terms of compatible size. The resulting bound no longer depends on $n$ and tends to zero as a tail of a convergent series. The professor explained that one can design such a proof by first leaving the thresholds generic, seeing which quantities need to balance, and then returning to choose the displayed $a_q$.

# 4. The Surviving Chain and Completion - §2.5.2, Proof of Theorem 2.5.6, p. 132

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Part 3.** Nestedness gives

$$
|\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f|A_{q-1}f
\le\Delta_{q-1}f A_{q-1}f
\le\sqrt n a_{q-1}.
$$

Also, by the choice of the partitions,

$$
\|\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f\|_{P,2}\le2^{-q+1}.
$$

Use Bernstein's inequality and the finite-class maximal bound again:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E^*\left\|\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\mathbb G_n\bigl((\pi_qf-\pi_{q-1}f)A_{q-1}f\bigr)\right\|_{\mathcal F}\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty
\left[
\frac{\sqrt n a_{q-1}}{\sqrt n}\log N_q
+2^{-q+1}\sqrt{\log N_q}
\right]\\
&\quad\le K\sum_{q=q_0+1}^\infty2^{-q}\sqrt{\log N_q}
\longrightarrow0\qquad(q_0\to\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

*Correction: The handwritten $L_2(P)$ bound has $2^{-(q+1)}$. The two representatives lie in the same parent cell at level $q-1$, so the bound is $2^{-(q-1)}=2^{-q+1}$, as stated in the transcript and the proof on p. 132.*

*Textbook clarification: The product functions are determined by the level-$q$ cells because the partitions are nested, so their cardinality is controlled by $N_q$. The $2^{-q+1}$ factor comes from the diameter bound at the parent level; it is absorbed into the universal constant (p. 132).*

Together the three parts establish the required approximation and asymptotic tightness, completing the Donsker theorem.

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The third term has the same structure as the second but needs only the two Bernstein contributions. Its supremum bound comes from the stopping test, and its $L_2$ bound comes from the ordinary covering part of the partition construction. The professor left the repeated verification of the Bernstein parameters abbreviated rather than repeating the whole second-term calculation.

The proof combines a carefully constructed chain, a tail inequality, and a maximal inequality for increasingly large finite sets. The entropy integrals control the resulting series. The professor contrasted the two Donsker approaches: the uniform-entropy argument uses conditional Rademacher sub-Gaussian bounds and $\psi_2$ control, while the present argument uses Bernstein bounds and $\psi_1$ control. In applications one chooses the theorem whose conditions are easier to verify for the class at hand.
