# 1. Unconditional Multiplier Central Limit Theorem - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.9, Theorem 2.9.2, p. 179

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 2.9. Multiplier CLT

**Theorem.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a class of measurable functions. Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n$ be i.i.d. random variables with

$$
E\xi_i=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_i)=1,
\qquad \|\xi_i\|_{2,1}<\infty,
$$

independent of $X_1,\ldots,X_n$. Then the sequence

$$
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i(\delta_{X_i}-P)
$$

converges weakly to a tight limit in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$ if and only if $\mathcal F$ is Donsker.

The limit is a $P$-Brownian bridge.

# 2. Joint Convergence to Independent Brownian Bridges - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.9, Corollary 2.9.3, p. 180

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Remark.** The pair

$$
\left(
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\delta_{X_i}-P),
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i(\delta_{X_i}-P)
\right)
$$

is asymptotically tight. The coordinates are uncorrelated. The joint marginals converge to multivariate normal distributions.

*Textbook clarification: These conclusions concern a Donsker class $\mathcal F$ under the multiplier assumptions of Theorem 2.9.2. The notes say that the marginals converge to normal distributions; joint multivariate normal convergence is what gives independence of the limiting bridges from the zero cross-covariances (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.9, p. 180).*

**Corollary.** Under the conditions of the preceding theorem, with $\mathcal F$ Donsker,

$$
\left(
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\delta_{X_i}-P),
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i(\delta_{X_i}-P)
\right)
\rightsquigarrow (G,G')
$$

in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)\times\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$, where $G,G'$ are two independent tight Brownian bridges.

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

# 3. Multipliers with Nonzero Mean - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.9, Corollary 2.9.4, pp. 180-181

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Consider $\xi_i$ with

$$
E\xi_i=\mu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_i)=\sigma^2.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\xi_i\delta_{X_i}-\mu P)
&=\frac\mu{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\delta_{X_i}-P)\\
&\quad+\frac\sigma{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\left(\frac{\xi_i-\mu}{\sigma}\right)(\delta_{X_i}-P)
+\sqrt n(\bar\xi_n-\mu)P.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last term is in $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$ when

$$
\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty.
$$

*Textbook clarification: The displayed standardization is for $\sigma>0$. The boundedness condition $\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty$ makes the random scalar multiple of $P$ an element of $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$, as used in Corollary 2.9.4 (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.9, p. 180).*

**Corollary.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a Donsker class with $\|P\|_{\mathcal F}<\infty$. Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n$ be i.i.d. with

$$
E\xi_i=\mu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_i)=\sigma^2,
\qquad \|\xi_i\|_{2,1}<\infty,
$$

independent of $X_1,\ldots,X_n$. Then

$$
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\xi_i\delta_{X_i}-\mu P)
\rightsquigarrow \mu G+\sigma G'+\sigma ZP,
$$

where $G,G'$ are independent tight Brownian bridges, independent of the random variable $Z\sim N(0,1)$.

The limiting Gaussian process $L=\mu G+\sigma G'+\sigma ZP$ has mean zero and covariance function

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(L(f),L(g))
=(\sigma^2+\mu^2)P(fg)-\mu^2(Pf)(Pg).
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The professor asked what changes when the multipliers are not standardized. The decomposition is obtained by adding and subtracting the appropriate terms; expanding its right-hand side is a direct way to check the cancellations. It separates the ordinary empirical process, a process with standardized multipliers, and a scalar sample-mean fluctuation, so the preceding results can be applied to these pieces.

# 4. Finite-Dimensional Conditional Multiplier Central Limit Theorem - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.9, Lemma 2.9.5, p. 181

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Conditional multiplier CLT

**Lemma (finite $\mathcal F$).** Let $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots$ be i.i.d. Euclidean random vectors with

$$
EZ_i=0,\qquad E\|Z_i\|^2<\infty,
$$

independent of the i.i.d. sequence $\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots$, with

$$
E\xi_i=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_i)=1.
$$

Then, conditionally on $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots$,

$$
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_iZ_i
\rightsquigarrow N(0,\operatorname{Cov}(Z_1))
$$

for almost every sequence $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots$.

*Correction: My notes say "for every sequence." This is "for almost every sequence," as stated in Lemma 2.9.5 (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.9, p. 181).*

**Proof.** Use the law of large numbers and the CLT with the Lindeberg conditions:

$$
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Z_iZ_i^\top
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cov}(Z_1),
$$

and, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\|Z_i\|^2
E_\xi\left[\xi_i^2\,
I\left(|\xi_i|\|Z_i\|>\varepsilon\sqrt n\right)\right]
\longrightarrow0.
$$

*Correction: My notes omit the absolute value around $\xi_i$ in the Lindeberg indicator. The threshold is on $|\xi_i|\|Z_i\|$, as in the proof of Lemma 2.9.5 (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.9, p. 181).*

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

This lemma is the finite-class starting point for the conditional theorem. The professor described it as an extension of the classical central limit theorem: after conditioning, the proof uses the law of large numbers and checks the covariance and Lindeberg conditions for the resulting weighted vector sum. Only this outline, rather than a detailed verification of the truncation condition, was given.

# 5. Bounded Lipschitz Distance - van der Vaart & Wellner §1.12, Theorem 1.12.4, p. 73; §2.9, p. 181

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Chapter 1.12

$$
G_n\rightsquigarrow G\quad\text{in }\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_{h\in BL_1}\left|E^\star h(G_n)-Eh(G)\right|\longrightarrow0,
$$

where

$$
BL_1=
\left\{
 h:\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)\to[0,1]:
 |h(z_1)-h(z_2)|\le\|z_1-z_2\|_{\mathcal F}
 \text{ for all }z_1,z_2
\right\}.
$$

*Textbook clarification: The limit $G$ is Borel measurable and separable. This qualification is needed in the stated bounded Lipschitz characterization (van der Vaart & Wellner, §1.12, p. 73; §2.9, p. 181).*

Weak convergence of separable Borel probability measures on a metric space is metrizable:

$$
\delta_{BL}(L_1,L_2)
:=\sup_{h\in BL_1}
\left|\int h\,dL_1-\int h\,dL_2\right|.
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The professor returned to a result from Chapter 1.12 that had been postponed earlier. The bounded Lipschitz test functions take an entire element of the function space as their input and return a number. Their Lipschitz condition is measured using the supremum norm on that space.

The important point is that weak convergence can be expressed as convergence to zero of a distance between laws. This offers another way to formulate and prove convergence results, rather than working directly with individual test-function limits. The characterization was stated without proof because its immediate purpose was to formulate the conditional multiplier theorem.

# 6. Conditional Multiplier Central Limit Theorem in Probability - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.9, Theorem 2.9.6, p. 182

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Theorem.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a class of measurable functions. Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n$ be i.i.d. with

$$
E\xi_i=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_i)=1,
\qquad \|\xi_i\|_{2,1}<\infty,
$$

independent of $X_1,\ldots,X_n$. Let

$$
G_n'=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i(\delta_{X_i}-P).
$$

Then the following are equivalent:

**(i)** $\mathcal F$ is Donsker.

**(ii)**

$$
\sup_{h\in BL_1}\left|E_\xi h(G_n')-Eh(G)\right|
\xrightarrow{P^\star }0,
$$

and $G_n'$ is asymptotically measurable:

$$
E^\star h(G_n')-E_\star h(G_n')\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for all }h\in C_b\bigl(\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)\bigr).
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

There are **two sources of randomness** in the multiplier process: the original observations and the multipliers. The earlier theorem treats them jointly. In the conditional result, the data are held fixed and the expectation averages only over the multipliers. The professor also distinguished the asymptotic-measurability condition, which uses all bounded continuous functionals, from the supremum over the smaller bounded Lipschitz class.

The motivation is bootstrap inference. In practice one has a fixed observed data set and then introduces another random mechanism through resampling, reweighting, or reshuffling. One wants to know whether the conditional distribution generated by that mechanism approximates the distribution relevant for the statistic, so it can be used for inference without explicitly calculating the limiting law. The professor said that the extra bootstrap randomness would later be related to multipliers. This makes the conditional result more delicate than a statement in which both the original data and the added randomness vary jointly.

# 7. Proof of the Conditional Multiplier Theorem - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.9, Theorem 2.9.6, pp. 182-183

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Proof of (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii).** By the multiplier CLT,

$$
G_n'\rightsquigarrow G',
$$

where $G'$ is a Brownian bridge. Thus $G_n'$ is asymptotically measurable.

$\mathcal F$ is $\rho_P$-totally bounded. For every $\delta>0$ and $f\in\mathcal F$, let $\Pi_\delta f$ be a closest element in a given finite $\delta$-net for $\mathcal F$ in $\rho_P$.

Almost surely, the sample paths of $G'$ are uniformly $\rho_P$-continuous, so

$$
\|G'\circ\Pi_\delta-G'\|_{\mathcal F}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{almost surely as }\delta\downarrow0.
$$

*Textbook clarification: The convergence is in the supremum norm on $\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)$, using uniform $\rho_P$-continuity of the Brownian-bridge paths, not merely pointwise continuity (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.1.2, p. 89; proof of Theorem 2.9.6, p. 182).*

Hence

$$
\sup_{h\in BL_1}
\left|Eh(G'\circ\Pi_\delta)-Eh(G')\right|
\longrightarrow0.
$$

By the lemma for finite $\mathcal F$, for every fixed $\delta>0$,

$$
\sup_{h\in BL_1}
\left|E_\xi h(G_n'\circ\Pi_\delta)-Eh(G'\circ\Pi_\delta)\right|
\longrightarrow0
$$

for almost all sequences $X_1,X_2,\ldots$.

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E_X^\star\sup_{h\in BL_1}
\left|E_\xi h(G_n'\circ\Pi_\delta)-E_\xi h(G_n')\right|\\
&\quad\le
E_X^\star\sup_{h\in BL_1}E_\xi
\left|h(G_n'\circ\Pi_\delta)-h(G_n')\right|\\
&\quad\le
E_X^\star E_\xi\|G_n'\circ\Pi_\delta-G_n'\|_{\mathcal F}^\star\\
&\quad\le E_X^\star E_\xi\|G_n'\|_{\mathcal F_\delta}^\star .
\end{aligned}
$$

Here

$$
\mathcal F_\delta
=\{f-g:f,g\in\mathcal F,\ \rho_P(f-g)<\delta\},
$$

and

$$
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
E^\star\|G_n'\|_{\mathcal F_\delta}=0.
$$

*Correction: The first line of this last handwritten bound repeats the projected-limit comparison, but the next line bounds the difference between $G_n'\circ\Pi_\delta$ and $G_n'$. The second term in the first line has therefore been corrected to $E_\xi h(G_n')$. The convergence is as $n\to\infty$ followed by $\delta\downarrow0$, not as $n\to\infty$ alone with arbitrary fixed $\delta$ (van der Vaart & Wellner, proof of Theorem 2.9.6, p. 182).*

*Textbook clarification: Measurable majorants are used for the possibly nonmeasurable norms in the conditional bound. The textbook bounds the outer expectation by $E^\star\|G_n'\|_{\mathcal F_\delta}$ and then applies the multiplier inequalities. Stars have been retained here to make this step precise (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.9, p. 182).*

Combining the three comparisons gives (ii).

**Proof of (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i).** Let $h(G_n')^\star $ and $h(G_n')_\star $ denote measurable majorants and minorants with respect to

$$
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n,X_1,\ldots,X_n)
$$

jointly. By the triangle inequality and Fubini's theorem,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|E^\star h(G_n')-Eh(G)\right|
&\le
\left|E_XE_\xi h(G_n')^\star -E_X^\star E_\xi h(G_n')\right|\\
&\quad+E_X^\star\left|E_\xi h(G_n')-Eh(G)\right|.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second term tends to zero for every $h\in BL_1$. The first is bounded by

$$
E_XE_\xi h(G_n')^\star -E_XE_\xi h(G_n')_\star
\longrightarrow0.
$$

*Textbook clarification: The second term tends to zero by boundedness and conditional convergence in outer probability. The first tends to zero by asymptotic measurability. The textbook then concludes unconditional convergence and uses the converse of Theorem 2.9.2 (van der Vaart & Wellner, §2.9, pp. 182-183).*

Thus $G_n'\rightsquigarrow G$ unconditionally. By the multiplier CLT, $\mathcal F$ is Donsker.

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The professor presented the proof as an example of working through the bounded Lipschitz distance. Total boundedness supplies finite approximations of the function class, where the finite-dimensional conditional lemma applies. The remaining comparison uses a bound already established in the proof of the unconditional multiplier theorem. The three comparisons are combined by the triangle inequality; the lecture did not expand every intermediate step of that combination.

For the reverse implication, the professor emphasized the bookkeeping between expectations over the data, expectations over the multipliers, and outer and inner expectations. Measurable majorants and minorants are taken with respect to both sources of randomness jointly. The asymptotic-measurability assumption controls their gap, allowing the conditional statement to yield unconditional convergence and hence the Donsker property.

The professor contrasted this style of proof with arguments organized around covering numbers and chaining, and briefly mentioned majorizing measures as another approach. No theorem or construction involving majorizing measures was developed. Another conditional multiplier theorem in the chapter was mentioned at the end but was not stated or proved; the lecture concluded with a reminder that conditional multiplier results would be needed for the bootstrap.
