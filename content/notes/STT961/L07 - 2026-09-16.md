# 1. Orlicz Norms and Examples - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.2, p. 95

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

### Ch. 2.2. Maximal Inequalities

**Def.** Let $\psi$ be a nondecreasing convex function on $\mathbb R^+$ with $\psi(0)=0$. For a random variable $X$, define the **Orlicz norm**

$$
\|X\|_\psi
:=\inf\left\{c>0:E\psi\left(\frac{|X|}{c}\right)\leq1\right\}.
$$

$$
\inf\varnothing=\infty.
$$

**Homework.** Check the three norm properties on $\{X:\|X\|_\psi<\infty\}$.

*Textbook clarification: To obtain a norm, $\psi$ must not be identically zero, and random variables equal almost surely are identified. The nonzero requirement is explicit in Lemma 2.2.2, p. 96; the definition and norm discussion are on p. 95.*

**Example 1.** $\psi(x)=x^p$, $p\geq1$. For $x>0$,

$$
\psi'(x)=px^{p-1}>0,
\qquad \psi''(x)=p(p-1)x^{p-2}\geq0.
$$

*Correction: My notes write $\psi''(x)>0$. At $p=1$ this second derivative is zero; convexity requires nonnegativity, not strict positivity. This preserves the $p\geq1$ range in §2.2, p. 95.*

$$
E\psi\left(\frac{|X|}{c}\right)
=E\left(\frac{|X|}{c}\right)^p\leq1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(E|X|^p)^{1/p}\leq c.
$$

Thus

$$
\|X\|_\psi=(E|X|^p)^{1/p}=\|X\|_{L^p}.
$$

**Example 2.** $\psi_p(x)=e^{x^p}-1$, $p\geq1$.

For $x>0$,

$$
\psi_p'(x)=e^{x^p}px^{p-1}>0,
$$

$$
\psi_p''(x)=e^{x^p}(px^{p-1})^2
+e^{x^p}p(p-1)x^{p-2}>0,
\qquad \psi_p(0)=0.
$$

$$
\|X\|_{\psi_p}
=\inf\left\{c>0:E\exp\left(\frac{|X|^p}{c^p}\right)\leq2\right\}.
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The professor introduced these norms as tools for bounding the suprema that arise in asymptotic equicontinuity. The admissible set in the infimum can be empty, in which case the norm is infinite. The homework is to check homogeneity, the triangle inequality, and the zero-norm property, using the assumptions on $\psi$.

The power example recovers a familiar $L^p$ norm. The exponential example will be more useful for the maximal inequalities in this course, because it puts stronger weight on the tails. Writing the exponential expectation as at most two is just the same definition with the minus one moved to the other side.

# 2. Norm Comparisons and Tail Bounds - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.2, pp. 95-96

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Properties.**

**0.** For $x\geq0$,

$$
x^p\leq e^{x^p}-1,
$$

so

$$
\|X\|_{L^p}\leq\|X\|_{\psi_p},\qquad p\geq1.
$$

**1.**

$$
\|X\|_{\psi_p}
\leq(\log2)^{1/q-1/p}\|X\|_{\psi_q},
\qquad1\leq p\leq q.
$$

*Textbook clarification: The handwritten exponent is $1/q-1/p$, which is consistent with the definitions. The supplied printing on p. 95 instead displays $p/q$; that cannot be right, since $p=q$ would give $\|X\|_{\psi_p}\leq(\log2)\|X\|_{\psi_p}$ for a nonzero $X$. The handwritten form is retained rather than replaced by that apparent printing error. It follows from concavity of $u^{p/q}$ applied at $u=\log2$ and the defining exponential-moment bound.*

**2.**

$$
\|X\|_{L^p}\leq p!\,\|X\|_{\psi_1}.
$$

*Textbook clarification: Here the factorial form is read for positive integer $p$; see the comparison on p. 95. For such $p$, the exponential series even gives the stronger factor $(p!)^{1/p}$, so the displayed lecture bound is valid.*

**3.** Put $a=\|X\|_\psi$, with $0<a<\infty$. For $x>0$ with $\psi(x/a)>0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(|X|>x)
&=P\left(\frac{|X|}{a}>\frac{x}{a}\right)\\
&\leq P\left\{\psi\left(\frac{|X|}{a}\right)\geq\psi\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)\right\}\\
&\leq\frac{E\psi(|X|/a)}{\psi(x/a)}
\leq\frac1{\psi(x/a)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

(Markov's inequality.)

*Correction: My last displayed step is written with an equality, but the definition gives $E\psi(|X|/a)\leq1$, not necessarily equality. Also, for a merely nondecreasing $\psi$, the transformed event is safely written with $\geq$; strict monotonicity is not assumed. The resulting tail bound is the one in §2.2, p. 96.*

Take $\psi=\psi_p=e^{x^p}-1$, $p\geq1$:

$$
P(|X|>x)
\leq\frac1{\exp\{(x/\|X\|_{\psi_p})^p\}-1}.
$$

This has an exponential tail of the form $e^{-Cx^p}$ for large $x$ when $\|X\|_{\psi_p}<\infty$.

*Correction: My notes write $P(|X|<x)$ in the exponential example. The calculation concerns the upper tail $P(|X|>x)$, as in §2.2, p. 96. The expression is a bound with exponential large-$x$ behavior, not an asserted equality for the distribution's tail.*

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The comparisons are useful up to fixed constants. A bound on the appropriate exponential Orlicz norm can yield bounds on ordinary moments, while retaining information about tail decay.

The professor derived the tail bound by inserting the norm into the denominator and applying Markov's inequality after the monotone transformation. The opposite direction will also hold for exponential tails. These norms were chosen to capture exponential and Gaussian-type tail behavior, not merely to introduce a more abstract notation.

# 3. From Exponential Tails to an Orlicz Norm - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.2, Lemma 2.2.1, p. 96

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Lemma.** Let $X$ be a random variable with

$$
P(|X|>x)\leq K e^{-Cx^p},\qquad x>0,
$$

where $K>0$, $C>0$, and $p\geq1$. Then

$$
\|X\|_{\psi_p}\leq\left(\frac{1+K}{C}\right)^{1/p}.
$$

**Proof.** Consider $E(e^{D|X|^p}-1)$, where $D=c^{-p}$. For $0<D<C$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(e^{D|X|^p}-1)
&=E\int_0^{|X|^p}De^{Ds}\,ds\\
&=E\int_0^\infty De^{Ds}I(s<|X|^p)\,ds\\
&=\int_0^\infty De^{Ds}P(s<|X|^p)\,ds\\
&=\int_0^\infty De^{Ds}P(|X|>s^{1/p})\,ds\\
&\leq\int_0^\infty De^{Ds}Ke^{-Cs}\,ds\\
&=DK\int_0^\infty e^{(D-C)s}\,ds\\
&=\left.\frac{DK}{D-C}e^{(D-C)s}\right|_0^\infty
=\frac{DK}{C-D}.
\end{aligned}
$$

*Correction: The first two integral lines in my notes omit the outer $E$, even though their upper limits or indicators still depend on $X$. The expectations are retained above until Fubini/Tonelli replaces the expected indicator by its probability, as in the proof of Lemma 2.2.1, p. 96.*

We want this at most one:

$$
DK\leq C-D
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D(K+1)\leq C
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D\leq\frac{C}{K+1}.
$$

Thus

$$
\frac1{c^p}\leq\frac{C}{K+1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c\geq\left(\frac{1+K}{C}\right)^{1/p}.
$$

Taking the infimum gives the claimed upper bound.

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The useful trick is to write the exponential minus one as an integral of its derivative, then replace a random upper integration limit by an indicator over the whole positive half-line. Interchanging expectation and integration exposes the tail probability, to which the assumed bound can be applied.

The resulting norm bound is an inequality because the proof constructs sufficient admissible constants; the true infimum could be smaller. The professor also cautioned that the textbook uses several similar-looking constants for different purposes. Here $c$ is the scale in the Orlicz definition and $C$ is the constant in the tail bound.

The recording suggests that a tail assumption only for sufficiently large $x$ can also be useful. That weaker assumption implies finiteness after adjusting constants, but it does not justify the displayed numerical bound with the same $K,C$ unchanged. The lemma above retains its stated all-$x>0$ hypothesis.

# 4. A Crude Maximal Inequality - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.2, p. 96

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

Consider the $L^p$ case: “replace max by sum” trick.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\max_{1\leq i\leq m}X_i\right\|_{L^p}
&=\left(E\left|\max_{1\leq i\leq m}X_i\right|^p\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\left(E\sum_{i=1}^m|X_i|^p\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\left(m\max_{1\leq i\leq m}E|X_i|^p\right)^{1/p}\\
&=m^{1/p}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\|X_i\|_{L^p}.
\end{aligned}
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

A maximal inequality tries to relate the norm of a maximum to the maximum of the individual norms. Replacing a maximum by a sum is a crude but very general method that needs no independence assumption. Its cost is the factor $m^{1/p}$, which becomes troublesome as the size of the collection grows. The exponential Orlicz norms will improve that growth to a logarithmic factor.

# 5. The Orlicz Maximal Inequality - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.2, Lemma 2.2.2, pp. 96-97

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

**Lemma.** Let $\psi$ be a nondecreasing, convex, nonzero function with $\psi(0)=0$ and

$$
\limsup_{x,y\to\infty}\frac{\psi(x)\psi(y)}{\psi(cxy)}<\infty
\quad\text{for some }c>0.
$$

Then for any random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_m$,

$$
\left\|\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|X_i|\right\|_\psi
\leq K\psi^{-1}(m)\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\|X_i\|_\psi,
$$

for a constant $K$ that depends on $\psi$ only.

*Textbook clarification: The lecture statement is written with $\max_iX_i$, but the subsequent proof establishes the bound for $\max_i|X_i|$ as well. This is the stronger form explicitly used in the textbook proof on p. 97.*

**Example.** For $\psi=\psi_p=e^{x^p}-1$,

$$
e^{x^p}-1=m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e^{x^p}=m+1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^p=\log(m+1).
$$

Therefore

$$
\psi_p^{-1}(m)=\{\log(m+1)\}^{1/p}.
$$

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The important feature is that the constant depends only on the chosen function $\psi$, rather than on the individual distributions or the number of variables. The inverse of $\psi$ determines the price of moving the maximum outside the norm. For the exponential choice, a power of $\log(m+1)$ grows much more slowly than a power of $m$.

# 6. Beginning the Proof - van der Vaart & Wellner §2.2, proof of Lemma 2.2.2, p. 97

## Professor Wrote (verbatim)

First assume

$$
\psi(x)\psi(y)\leq\psi(cxy),\qquad x,y\geq1.
$$

Then

$$
\psi(x/y)\leq\frac{\psi(cx)}{\psi(y)},\qquad x\geq y\geq1.
$$

Consider any $y\geq1$ and $A>0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\psi\left(\frac{|X_i|}{Ay}\right)
&=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left[
\psi\left(\frac{|X_i|}{Ay}\right)
 I\left(\frac{|X_i|}{Ay}\geq1\right)
+\psi\left(\frac{|X_i|}{Ay}\right)
 I\left(\frac{|X_i|}{Ay}<1\right)
\right]\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^m\frac{\psi(c|X_i|/A)}{\psi(y)}+\psi(1).
\end{aligned}
$$

*Textbook clarification: These divisions are used for $\psi(y)>0$. The later choice $y=\psi^{-1}(2m)$ ensures this. The capital scale constant in the handwritten proof is renamed $A$ to distinguish it from $c$ in the hypothesis; the mathematical inequality is unchanged.*

**O.O.T.** [Out of time.]

---

## Professor Said (Paraphrased)

The proof temporarily strengthens the asymptotic condition on $\psi$ to a bound valid for every $x,y\geq1$. The professor said that this extra assumption would be removed at the end.

The free parameter $y$ is inserted so it can later be chosen in terms of the number of variables. Splitting according to whether the ratio is at least one permits the stronger product inequality to handle the large-ratio part, while monotonicity bounds the other part by $\psi(1)$. The maximum over the first part is then replaced by a sum.
